Drive the corridor north of the 101 and count the signs. The builders are not advertising discounts. They are advertising rates.

That is a choice, made deliberately, by companies with analytics departments. Understanding why they made it is the fastest way for a resale seller to stop losing to them.

## Reason one: the payment is what buyers actually shop

A buyer with a budget does not have a price ceiling. They have a payment ceiling, and they work backwards from it.

A builder advertising a rate in the fives is speaking directly to that ceiling. A resale listing advertising a price reduction is speaking to a number the buyer has to translate before it means anything, and most never do the translation.

Same money, different language. One of them is in the buyer's language.

## Reason two: price cuts damage the comparables, financing does not

This is the strategic reason and it is the one resale sellers underweight.

A builder selling eighty homes in a community cannot cut the price on home forty without affecting the appraised value of homes forty one through eighty. Every cut resets the comp set the rest of the community will be appraised against. Cut twice and the whole remaining inventory is worth less, including the lots they have not released yet.

A financing incentive does not show up in the comp set the same way. The recorded sale price holds. The incentive is a closing cost item. The builder protects the value of everything they have left to sell and still delivers the buyer a payment that wins.

Resale sellers have the same exposure and rarely think about it. If you are selling in a subdivision where your neighbors are also listing, or where you are buying your next house, the price you record matters to more people than you. See [Seller Concession vs Price Reduction](/blog/seller-concession-vs-price-reduction-math/).

## Reason three: the leverage per dollar is better

A dollar of price reduction removes a dollar from the loan balance. A dollar spent subsidizing the rate moves the payment several times further, because the payment is mostly a function of the rate, not the balance.

A builder spending a fixed incentive budget gets more visible buyer benefit per dollar out of financing than out of price, every time. That is arithmetic, not marketing.

## Reason four: they control the lender

Most large builders have an affiliated mortgage company, and that integration does real work. The sales office can quote a payment on the spot, lock it, and stand behind it, because the lender is down the hall. There is no coordination problem, no waiting for a callback, no quoting a rate that may not be there Monday.

This is the one structural advantage resale sellers have to engineer around rather than simply copy. ## What the builder is not telling the buyer

Worth knowing, because it is the counter-argument a resale listing can make honestly:

**The incentive is usually tied to using their lender.** Which means the buyer is not shopping that loan. The rate looks good; whether the overall cost is good is a separate question the buyer never gets to test. See [Builder's Lender or Your Own](/blog/builder-lender-vs-your-own-lender/).

**The price is held, which means the buyer pays full price.** They receive financing help and record a sale at the number the builder wanted. On a resale, a buyer can frequently negotiate both.

**New construction has a delivery timeline.** A buyer needs a rate that survives it, which is a real complication. See [Extended Rate Locks on New Construction](/blog/extended-rate-lock-new-construction/).

**Resale inventory is finished.** Mature landscaping, window coverings, no six month wait, and a known neighborhood. That is worth something a builder cannot offer, and it is worth more when the payment comparison is no longer lopsided.

## The play for a resale listing

1. **Stop competing on price with somebody who is competing on payment.** You will lose that comparison even when you win it, because the buyer is not scoring the same contest.
2. **Hold your price and fund a buydown instead.** Match the builder's number or beat it. See [The 3-2-1 Buydown](/blog/3-2-1-buydown-explained/).
3. **Lock the rate to the address before you list** so your marketing can state a number rather than imply one.
4. **Advertise the payment as the headline.** See [How Listing Agents Should Market the Payment](/blog/listing-agent-payment-marketing-playbook/).
5. **Then add the arguments the builder cannot make.** Finished home, established neighborhood, no construction timeline, and a buyer who was free to shop the loan.

That last point is the whole position. Once the payment comparison is even, everything else about a finished resale home in an established Scottsdale neighborhood is an advantage.
