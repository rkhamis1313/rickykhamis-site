Old Town Scottsdale's nightlife is concentrated into one district, and knowing exactly where it starts and stops is the most useful thing anyone can tell you, whether you are planning a night out or thinking about buying a place here.

The [Entertainment District](https://www.experiencescottsdale.com/stories/post/old-town-nightlife-hot-spots/) is anchored around Camelback Road and Saddlebag Trail, just east of Scottsdale Road in the northeast corner of Old Town. More than 100 bars, clubs and restaurants sit in a footprint tight enough to walk end to end. You can move from a rooftop happy hour to a dance floor to a late night taco without ever calling a ride.

That density is the appeal. It is also, precisely, the thing to understand before you book a hotel or buy a condo nearby.

## Rooftops

**Outrider Rooftop Lounge**, on top of the Canopy by Hilton Scottsdale Old Town, is the one people name first. Views across Old Town, and busy enough at sunset that you should not arrive at seven expecting a table.

**Cottontail Lounge**, on the second floor of the W Scottsdale, sits right in the middle of the district with the WET Deck pool as its centerpiece.

**Estelle** is newer, a two-story open-air bar and dining space, and it is the addition people have been talking about most recently.

## Day drinking that becomes night drinking

**Maya Day + Nightclub** runs Vegas-style pool parties that roll straight into nightclub DJ sets after dark. If you have heard that Scottsdale does day clubs, this is what they meant.

## Bars with a crowd and a soundtrack

**Whiskey Row**, **Bottled Blonde**, **El Hefe**, **The Hot Chick** and **Hi-Fi Kitchen & Cocktails** are the names that come up repeatedly. Between them the district covers country through EDM, and the crowd shifts noticeably by night of the week.

## How to plan the night

**Start on a rooftop around sunset.** The light over Camelback is the reason to be outside at that hour, and the rooftops are calm before nine.

**Eat before, not during.** Dinner inside the district on a Saturday is a negotiation with the noise. Eat in the Arts District or along the canal, then walk over. It is five minutes.

**Thursday is the underrated night.** ArtWalk runs 7pm to 9pm in the Arts District, and you can do galleries and wine first, then the Entertainment District after, without either feeling rushed.

**Do not drive.** The whole point of this district is that you do not have to. It is walkable, and rideshare is everywhere.

**Know the season.** October through April is peak. Spring training, the WM Phoenix Open and Barrett-Jackson each turn the volume up further. July is a different town entirely.

## Now the part that costs people money

Here is the thing nobody tells buyers, and I tell every one of them:

**Walk the specific address on a Friday night before you buy it.** Not a Tuesday afternoon. Not on a map. Stand on the actual block at 11pm.

Old Town is compact enough that four blocks is the difference between a quiet residential street and a building that backs onto a district running until 2am. Both are legitimate places to own. Only one of them is what you pictured. Buyers tour on a weekday, fall in love with the walkability, and discover the rest at closing.

## What the nightlife does to a mortgage

Three things, all real.

**It drives the rental demand, and lenders discount it anyway.** Walkability to this district is exactly why Old Town short-term rental income is strong. It is also seasonal and event-driven, and a lender underwrites a twelve month average rather than your six best weeks. Most will not use projected nightly income at all, and the ones who will apply a serious haircut. That gap is the whole game here. See [Spring Training, the Open, and Your Loan](/blog/scottsdale-short-term-rental-income-loan/).

**The association may settle the question before the city does.** Scottsdale regulates short-term rentals with registration, licensing, notification and insurance requirements. Your building's association can restrict or prohibit rentals independently, and commonly sets a minimum lease term. Thirty days or more ends a nightly rental plan no matter what the city permits. The stricter rule governs. Get both in writing before you remove contingencies.

**Buildings near the district skew investor-heavy, and that fails projects.** Condominium financing underwrites the building as well as the buyer. A high share of non-owner-occupants restricts or fails a project on agency financing, and it is a common finding in exactly this part of Old Town. A building running hotel-style services can be classified as a condotel and fall outside agency financing entirely. See [Scottsdale Luxury Condo Financing](/blog/scottsdale-luxury-condo-project-approval/).

And if you are telling a lender it is a second home while renting it nightly most of the year, that is not a labeling choice. See [Second Home or Investment Property in Scottsdale](/blog/scottsdale-second-home-vs-investment/).

## The short version

The Entertainment District is genuinely good at what it does, and it is the engine under Old Town's rental market. Buy near it on purpose, with the association's rental policy and the project's owner-occupancy ratio in hand, and it is a strong asset. Buy near it by accident and you will learn all of this the expensive way.
