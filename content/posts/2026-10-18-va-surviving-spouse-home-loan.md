This is the benefit I see claimed least often, and the one where not knowing costs the most.

If your spouse died in service, or died from a condition connected to their service, you may be eligible for the VA home loan in your own right. Not as a co-borrower. As the eligible party.

## What the benefit covers

[[RULE:va-surviving-spouse]]

The categories generally contemplated are a surviving spouse of a service member who died in the line of duty, and a surviving spouse of a veteran whose death resulted from a service-connected disability. There are also provisions touching service members missing in action or held as prisoners of war.

Whether remarriage affects eligibility depends on the circumstances and the rules in force, which is exactly why this is a question for the VA rather than for an internet page, including this one.

## Why it matters so much

**No down payment.** The same zero-down financing any eligible borrower gets.

[[RULE:va-no-down-payment]]

**No monthly mortgage insurance.** Over a thirty year loan this is a very large number compared with the FHA alternative a surviving spouse would otherwise be steered toward.

[[RULE:va-no-mortgage-insurance]]

**The funding fee exemption.** This is the piece to pay attention to.

[[RULE:va-funding-fee]]

An eligible surviving spouse is generally exempt from the VA funding fee. On a typical purchase that is thousands of dollars that simply are not charged, and I have seen it missed by lenders who processed the file as an ordinary VA loan without checking.

**No VA credit score minimum**, which can matter a great deal after a period when finances were the last thing anyone was thinking about.

[[RULE:va-no-minimum-credit-score]]

## How to establish it

You need a Certificate of Eligibility in your own name.

[[RULE:va-certificate-of-eligibility]]

What is generally needed:

- Your spouse's **DD-214** or discharge documents
- The **death certificate**
- Your **marriage certificate**
- Where death resulted from a service-connected condition, **documentation of that connection**, which is frequently the VA's own disability determination
- If you receive **Dependency and Indemnity Compensation**, documentation of that, since it speaks directly to the service connection

A lender can usually assist with the application. If the service connection has not been established, that is a VA claims matter and worth pursuing with a Veterans Service Officer, who does this as part of their role. That process takes time, so start it before you need it rather than during an escrow.

## Income that may count

Two things worth raising with your lender, because they are routinely overlooked:

**Dependency and Indemnity Compensation** is generally non-taxable, and non-taxable income can be grossed up on many programs, which improves your qualifying ratio. Ask specifically whether they are doing that.

**Survivor Benefit Plan annuity payments** are documentable continuing income.

And VA's residual income test, which scales with household size, frequently treats a surviving spouse household more favorably than a pure debt-to-income calculation would.

[[RULE:va-residual-income]]

## If you were told you are not eligible

Get the determination from the VA rather than from a lender's assumption. The rules here are specific and plenty of originators have never processed one of these files.

A Veterans Service Officer can help with the eligibility and claims side, and that service is free. That is worth a phone call before you accept anybody's no.
