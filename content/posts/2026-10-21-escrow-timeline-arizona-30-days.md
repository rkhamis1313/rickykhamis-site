Contracts get written with thirty day closings because thirty days sounds decisive. On a clean conventional file with a cooperative appraisal, thirty days works. On a self-employed jumbo file secured by a property with a well, a septic system and two associations, thirty days is a fiction that everyone discovers in week three.

Here is what actually happens, and what to negotiate instead.

## Week one is the week that decides everything

Almost every delayed closing I have seen traces back to something that should have happened in week one and did not. Front-load aggressively.

**Order the appraisal immediately.** Not after the inspection. Not after the inspection period closes. Immediately. The appraisal is frequently the longest lead item and it gates the loan.

**Open escrow and order the title commitment.** Title issues need runway. A clouded title, an unreleased lien, an easement problem or a probate matter in the chain takes weeks, not days.

**Schedule the home inspection**, and on a septic property, **schedule the septic transfer inspection now.** Qualified inspectors have queues, the tank generally has to be pumped and opened, and that is two vendors in sequence. See [The Arizona Septic Transfer Inspection](/blog/arizona-septic-transfer-inspection-loan/).

**On a well, start the testing** and get any shared well agreement to your lender. See [Well Water and Mortgage Financing in Arizona](/blog/well-water-mortgage-arizona/).

**Request the association documents.** Associations take as long as they take. On a property with multiple associations, multiply. See [When HOA Documents Delay a Closing](/blog/hoa-document-delays-closing/).

**On a condominium, start project review.** This can disqualify the property entirely and you want to know in week one, not week four. See [Condo and Project Approval](/blog/scottsdale-luxury-condo-project-approval/).

**Submit your full document package to the lender.** All of it, at once. A file submitted in pieces gets underwritten in pieces.

**Lock your rate, or make a deliberate decision not to.** See [Lock or Float](/blog/rate-lock-vs-float-arizona/).

## Week two: underwriting and findings

The underwriter reads the file and issues conditions. Expect conditions. A file with no conditions is rare and a file with many is normal, not a sign of trouble.

Inspection results arrive and any repair negotiation begins. The appraisal may come in; if it does and the value is short, this is where you deal with it. See [When the Appraisal Comes In Low](/blog/appraisal-gap-arizona-contract/).

**The one behavior that matters:** respond to conditions within a day. Underwriting queues are first in, first out. A document that sits on your desk for three days costs you considerably more than three days, because you go back into the queue behind everyone else.

## Week three: conditions cleared, or the date starts moving

Repair negotiations resolve and any agreed repairs get completed and documented. Remaining conditions clear. Title issues, if there are any, are either resolved or now visibly threatening the date.

This is the week where a thirty day close either holds or does not. If a major item is still open at the end of week three, start the conversation about extending rather than hoping.

## Week four: final approval and closing

Final approval, closing disclosure delivered with the required waiting period before signing, final employment and credit verification, wire instructions, signing and recording.

Two things to know about this week:

**There is a mandatory waiting period** between delivery of the Closing Disclosure and consummation. It is short but it is real, and certain late changes restart it. Which is a reason not to make changes in week four.

**Your credit and employment get re-verified.** A new credit account or a job change discovered here can stop the closing cold. See the list of things not to do below.

## The five items that most commonly move a closing date

**1. The appraisal was ordered late, or came in short.** Order it in week one and the first half of this disappears.

**2. Septic or well documentation was not scheduled early.** Entirely preventable, and routinely not prevented.

**3. Association documents or a condominium project review.** Outside your control, which is exactly why it has to start in week one.

**4. Borrower response time on conditions.** The one variable you fully control and the one most often blamed on the lender.

**5. Title problems.** Liens, easements, probate matters, a name mismatch in the chain. These need weeks.

## What to negotiate instead of thirty days

**Clean conventional resale, municipal water and sewer, one association, W-2 income:** thirty days is realistic.

**Self-employed or non-QM income:** add a week. The income analysis itself takes real underwriting time.

**Jumbo:** add a week. More documentation, more scrutiny, frequently a second review.

**Well or septic:** add a week, possibly more. These are calendar items, not effort items, and you cannot compress a vendor queue.

**Condominium requiring project review:** add a week or two, and accept that the answer might be no.

**Custom home or large acreage:** add time for the appraisal, which is harder here and more likely to need a reconsideration.

**New construction:** a different animal entirely. See [Extended Rate Locks on New Construction](/blog/extended-rate-lock-new-construction/).

A forty day close that holds is worth more than a thirty day close that slips, to everyone in the transaction. Sellers understand this when it is explained.

## Do not disturb your file between application and closing

- **No new credit accounts.** Not a car, not furniture, not a store card.
- **No job changes** if avoidable, and tell your lender immediately if one is coming.
- **No large unexplained deposits.** Everything gets sourced. See [Large Deposits and NSF Items](/blog/large-deposits-nsf-bank-statements/).
- **Do not spend your reserves.** They are tested after closing.
- **Do not move money between accounts** without telling your lender, because it creates documentation work for no benefit.
- **Keep paying everything on time.**

## The one-sentence version

Front-load week one, respond to conditions within a day, and negotiate a closing date that reflects what is actually in your file rather than what sounds good in an offer.
