*Part of the [FHA loans in Arizona](/loan-programs/fha-loans/) guide, where every rule on this page is cited and dated.*

Support payments cut both ways in an FHA file, and borrowers routinely get one direction wrong.

If you receive support, it can be real qualifying income and people fail to claim it. If you pay support, it is an obligation and people fail to disclose it, which an underwriter finds anyway.

## Support you receive

[[RULE:fha-child-support-income]]

The general shape of what underwriting needs, with specifics varying by program:

**The order or agreement establishing it.** A decree, a court order, or a formal separation agreement. A verbal arrangement with a former spouse is not documentable income no matter how reliably it arrives.

**Evidence of actual receipt**, over a period. Bank statements showing deposits, or payment records from the state disbursement unit if payments route through one. In Arizona, support frequently runs through the state's clearinghouse, and those records are exactly the documentation underwriting wants. Get the payment history printout.

**A defined remaining duration.** This is the one that surprises people. Income that ends soon is worth little for qualifying a thirty year mortgage, and child support has an end date built in. If your youngest child is close to the age where support terminates, that income may not help you much or at all. Worth knowing before you build a budget on it.

**Consistency.** Sporadic or partial payments are a problem even with an order in place. An order saying one amount and a deposit history showing less is read against the deposit history.

One useful point: **some support income may be treated as non-taxable**, and non-taxable income can be grossed up on many programs, which improves your ratio. Ask whether that applies to your situation, because it is real purchasing power and it is frequently overlooked.

## Support you pay

[[RULE:fha-child-support-obligation]]

Shorter story: it is a monthly obligation and it goes into your debt-to-income ratio.

Three things worth knowing:

**Disclose it up front.** It appears in credit reporting, in the decree, and in a bank statement showing a recurring withdrawal. An underwriter finds it. A borrower who did not disclose it has a credibility problem on top of a ratio problem, and credibility matters in a manual underwrite.

**Arrears are a separate and more serious issue.** Being behind on support is not just a ratio item. Depending on the circumstances it can affect eligibility, and unpaid obligations that have been referred for collection can create other complications. Raise it on the first call rather than hoping.

**Treatment of an obligation that is ending can differ.** If your support obligation terminates within a defined period, ask specifically how your program treats that. It is worth asking rather than assuming it counts in full forever.

## Where your ratio stands

[[RULE:fha-dti-limits]]

Support in either direction moves this number directly, which is why getting both sides documented correctly matters more than almost anything else in a file where support is involved.

If the ratio is tight, the levers worth looking at before anything exotic:

- **Pay off revolving debt** rather than paying it down. No discretionary debt is a recognized compensating factor in a manual underwrite.
- **A permanent rate buydown**, which lowers the note rate and therefore the qualifying payment. A temporary buydown generally will not help, because you are qualified at the note rate. See [Permanent vs Temporary Buydown](/blog/permanent-vs-temporary-buydown/).
- **A larger down payment**, which reduces the payment directly.
- **Claiming income you have not claimed**, including gross-up treatment on non-taxable support.

## If you are in or just out of a divorce

Several things interact here and the sequence matters a great deal.

**A decree does not remove anyone from a mortgage.** If you are still on the loan for the marital home, that payment generally counts in your ratio when you buy, unless it is excluded under specific program conditions. Ask about those conditions specifically, because treatment varies between investors and it is one of the larger differences between lenders on a file like this.

**Talk to a lender before the decree is final.** The single most valuable thing on this page. How the decree handles the house, the buyout, and support can determine which financing treatments are available to you afterward. Your attorney drafts it; your lender can tell them what financing needs in order to work. Getting those two in one conversation early saves real money.

**Credit damage from the separation is common and fixable**, but only on the right timeline. Credit work done before you shop changes which programs exist for you; the same work after an offer is accepted changes nothing. See [Fix the Credit First](/blog/credit-repair-before-mortgage/).

**Expect a manual underwrite** if there was credit damage, and prepare the compensating factors. See [What a Manual Underwrite Is](/blog/fha-manual-underwrite-explained/).

The full picture is in [Divorce and the Mortgage in Arizona](/blog/divorce-mortgage-arizona/).

## What to gather

**If you receive support:**

- The decree or order, complete
- The payment history, ideally from the state disbursement records
- Bank statements showing the deposits
- The termination date for each child
- A question for your lender about gross-up treatment

**If you pay support:**

- The decree or order
- Proof of current status and that you are not in arrears
- The termination date
- A disclosure on the first call, not the third week
