Agritopia is the Johnston family's former farmland in Gilbert, redeveloped as a neighborhood built around a working urban farm rather than a golf course. Orchards and row crops inside the community, a commercial node with Joe's Farm Grill and the Coffee Shop, front porches, alleys, and a walkable layout that almost nothing else in the East Valley matches.

It attracts entrepreneurs and small business owners for the reason you would guess: the people who choose a neighborhood built on an idea tend to be people running something of their own. Which makes the financing conversation predictable, and it has two halves.

## Half one: how an underwriter rebuilds your income

Two years of filed returns, analyzed on Fannie Mae Form 1084 or Freddie Mac Form 91. Not revenue, not your profit and loss.

- **Schedule C:** net profit from line 31, plus depreciation, depletion and business use of home added back, minus non-recurring income, divided by 24.
- **1120S and K-1:** your W-2 wage from the entity counts. K-1 ordinary income counts only where distributions are documented and the business has liquidity to keep paying them.
- **1065 and K-1:** same, with guaranteed payments included.
- **Schedule E:** rental net with depreciation, taxes, insurance, interest and dues added back where allowed, minus full PITIA.

The arithmetic nobody enjoys: every dollar of aggressive deduction saves tax at your marginal rate and costs roughly four to five dollars of buying power. A year two lower than year one generally becomes your qualifying income rather than the average. See [Business Owner Write-Offs and Your Mortgage](/blog/business-owner-write-offs-mortgage/).

If you have been self-employed under two years, that is a specific problem with specific answers rather than a dead end. See [Recently Self-Employed](/blog/recently-self-employed-mortgage/).

## Half two: what Agritopia adds on the property side

**Housing types vary and so does the loan.** Single family homes, attached product, and condominium units including the Generation Ag development. A condominium purchase means the loan depends on the **project** qualifying as well as you: owner occupancy ratios, budget and reserve adequacy, insurance coverage, litigation, and single-owner concentration. A fully qualified buyer gets declined over a project they do not control. Establish the project's status before you write. See [Condo and Project Approval](/blog/scottsdale-luxury-condo-project-approval/).

**Community obligations are layered, and they count.** Between the association and the community's farm and amenity structure, the monthly obligation attached to an address here is not a single line. Every recurring obligation goes into your debt-to-income calculation, and on a self-employed file where the qualifying income was already rebuilt downward, that number matters more than usual. Get the complete figure for the specific address in writing, not an estimate. See [HOA and Club Dues in Your Ratio](/blog/scottsdale-hoa-club-dues-debt-to-income/).

**Appraisals here need a thoughtful appraiser.** Agritopia is not comparable to the Gilbert subdivision across the arterial, and the premium buyers pay for it is real but has to be supported by sales inside the community. A low volume of recent closings in a specific housing type stretches the comparable set. Order early.

**If you work from home, say so, but know what it does and does not mean.** Business use of home is an add-back on the income analysis. It is not a commercial use question on a residential loan unless you are doing something the property is not zoned for.

## When the returns do not support the purchase

Take conventional financing if your returns support it. Cheapest money available, and in Gilbert's price range most self-employed buyers who document properly can get there. When the returns genuinely do not support it, these are the alternatives, and none of them is a shortcut around qualifying.

**Bank statement.** Twelve or twenty four months of deposits with an expense factor applied by business type. Transfers between your own accounts are stripped out. See [How Bank Statement Income Is Calculated](/blog/bank-statement-loans-how-income-is-calculated/) and [12 vs 24 Month Bank Statements](/blog/12-vs-24-month-bank-statements/).

**Profit and loss or written verification of employment programs.** A CPA-prepared profit and loss, sometimes with supporting statements. See [P&L and WVOE Loans](/blog/profit-and-loss-wvoe-loans-self-employed/).

**Asset depletion**, where the business has made you asset-rich and income-light on paper.

**No-ratio**, where no debt-to-income test is applied at all and credit, assets, reserves and the property carry the file.

Note one interaction: **a non-QM loan on a condominium unit is two sets of eligibility requirements at once.** Raise both on the first call so nobody finds the overlap late.

## Before you write in Agritopia

- **Confirm the exact housing type** and whether the loan requires project review.
- **Get the complete monthly community obligation** for the specific address in writing.
- **Have your lender run both the agency analysis and a bank statement analysis** so you can see which produces the better approval before you shop.
- **Get your CPA and your lender in one conversation** before the qualifying returns are filed.
- **Count reserves the way a lender will:** after closing, excluding business funds you cannot document access to.
- **Get fully underwritten, not pre-qualified.** A pre-qualification is a calculator. An underwritten pre-approval means a human read your returns.
