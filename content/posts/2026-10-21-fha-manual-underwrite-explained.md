Most FHA files run through an automated evaluation and come out the other side. Some do not, and those get manually underwritten.

If you are in a manual underwrite, two things are true: your ratio tolerance is tighter, and the quality of how your file is presented genuinely affects whether it is approved. That second part is unusual in lending and it is worth understanding.

## The two paths

**TOTAL Mortgage Scorecard** is HUD's automated evaluation. A file that returns an acceptable result generally gets lighter documentation and more generous ratio tolerance.

**Manual underwriting** is a human applying a defined, stricter framework. You do not land here at random. There are specific triggers.

## Why you are probably in one

The usual reasons:

**A referral from the automated evaluation**, meaning it declined to render an acceptable result.

**No usable credit score, or a very thin file.** A borrower with little or no traditional credit cannot be scored, which routes to manual underwriting with alternative credit documentation. This catches people who have lived without debt on purpose and assume that is a strength.

**A recent significant credit event.** See [Getting an FHA Loan After a Bankruptcy or Foreclosure](/blog/fha-after-bankruptcy-arizona/).

**Recent housing payment lates.** Housing history gets weighted heavily.

**Ratios above what the automated path accepted.**

**Certain collection, judgment or disputed-account situations.** See [Collections, Judgments and Federal Debt](/blog/fha-collections-judgments-federal-debt/).

Ask your lender directly which one applies to you. The answer tells you what to work on.

## What changes

**Ratio limits tighten.**

[[RULE:fha-dti-limits]]

The gap between the automated tolerance and the manual limit is the thing that surprises borrowers. A file that was comfortable under the automated path can be over the limit under the manual one, with no change to your actual finances.

**Documentation increases.** Expect more of it, and expect verification of things the automated path would have accepted.

**Housing payment history becomes central.** A documented record of paying rent or a mortgage on time is among the most persuasive things in a manual file. Cancelled checks or bank statements showing consistent on-time payments carry real weight.

**Reserves start to matter a great deal.** On the automated path reserves are frequently incidental. In a manual underwrite they are one of the strongest compensating factors available to you. Cash in the bank after closing is persuasive in a way it simply is not elsewhere.

**Compensating factors become the argument.** This is the part worth understanding properly.

## Compensating factors, and why presentation matters

A manual underwrite allows an underwriter to approve a file that exceeds a baseline limit where documented compensating factors justify it. Which factors are acceptable, and how many are needed at a given ratio, is set out in the handbook.

The general categories:

**Verified reserves** after closing. Usually the most powerful one.

**Minimal increase in housing payment.** If your new payment is close to what you already demonstrably pay, that is a strong argument. A borrower going from a high rent to a similar mortgage payment is a different risk from one doubling their housing cost.

**Residual income**, meaning income left after all obligations. Particularly relevant for larger households.

**No discretionary debt**, where you carry no revolving balances.

**Significant additional income not used in qualifying.** Income that exists and was not counted, documented, is a real factor.

**A long, documented history of conservative credit use.**

Here is the practical point: **an underwriter can only credit a compensating factor they can see.** A borrower with two years of perfect rent payments and meaningful reserves has a strong file, and if nobody documents the rent history or the reserves properly, the underwriter is looking at a thin file with a high ratio.

That is why a manual underwrite is the one situation in lending where who presents your file genuinely changes the outcome. Not through persuasion, through documentation. The facts are the facts; whether they are in front of the decision-maker is a choice somebody makes.

## How to actually win one

**Document your housing history obsessively.** Twelve to twenty four months of cancelled checks or bank statements showing on-time rent. If you pay a private landlord, get a written verification and the bank records to support it. This is the highest-value document in a manual file.

**Build and hold reserves.** Do not spend your last dollar on the down payment. Reserves are tested after closing and in a manual underwrite they are your strongest factor. A buyer who covers an appraisal gap with their reserve position has traded their best argument for a few thousand dollars.

**Pay off revolving debt rather than paying it down.** No discretionary debt is a recognized factor. Carrying small balances across several cards is worse than clearing them.

**Identify income that is not being counted** and document it. Part-time work, a documented bonus history, support income. See [Child Support and Alimony on an FHA File](/blog/fha-child-support-alimony/).

**Write a clear, specific letter of explanation** for anything in your file that needs one. Specific and consistent with the documents, not vague and apologetic.

**If you have thin credit, build an alternative credit file.** Utilities, rent, insurance, phone, consistently paid and documentable. Start this months before you apply.

**Consider a permanent rate buydown.** A permanent buydown lowers the note rate, which lowers the qualifying payment, which lowers your ratio directly. In a manual underwrite with a tight ratio limit, that can be the whole difference. A temporary buydown generally will not help, because you are qualified at the note rate. See [Permanent vs Temporary Buydown](/blog/permanent-vs-temporary-buydown/).

**Allow more calendar time.** A manual underwrite takes longer. Negotiate a closing date that reflects that rather than hoping. See [A Realistic Escrow Timeline](/blog/escrow-timeline-arizona-30-days/).

## The thing to ask your lender

> *"Am I being manually underwritten, and if so, why? What ratio limit applies to me, and which compensating factors are you documenting?"*

If they cannot answer all three, find out before you are in contract. A manual underwrite handled by someone treating it as a normal file is how a borrower who should have been approved gets declined.
