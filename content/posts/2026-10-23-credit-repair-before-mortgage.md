A borrower came to me on a primary residence near Thompson Peak. Self-employed, real assets, a legitimate purchase, and a file that several lenders had already looked at and declined. Nobody could make the income math work.

Two things closed it, and the order they happened in was the whole thing.

**The credit got fixed first.** His report carried derogatory items that were either inaccurate or that the furnishers could not verify when challenged through the bureau dispute process. Working through those and letting the reporting correct moved him to a 770 FICO. That is not a trick and it is not fast. It is the Fair Credit Reporting Act operating as designed, and it takes weeks.

**Then the file got structured.** At 770 a no-ratio non-QM loan became available, on terms that were not available at his earlier score. No debt-to-income test at all: credit, assets, reserves and the property carried it. The structure that closed the loan only existed because the credit work happened first.

Had he found a house, written an offer, and then started on his credit, none of it would have worked. Not because the facts were different, but because thirty days is not enough time for credit reporting to correct.

## Why score thresholds matter more than people think

Pricing and eligibility are tiered, and the tiers are steps rather than a slope. Moving from one tier to the next changes:

**Pricing.** A score tier is worth real money in rate, every month, for as long as you hold the loan.

**Down payment requirements.** Particularly on non-QM and jumbo programs, where minimum down payment frequently steps with score.

**Program eligibility outright.** This is the one that matters most and gets discussed least. Some programs have a floor. Below it they do not exist for you at any price. A no-ratio loan is a good example: it is a program for strong credit, because credit is doing the work that a debt-to-income calculation does on an agency loan.

**Reserve requirements.** Often inversely related to score.

So a thirty point improvement is not a thirty point improvement in rate. It can be the difference between a structure existing and not existing.

## What legitimate credit work looks like

Nothing here is a loophole. It is using the rights the law gives you and doing the ordinary things in the right order.

**Dispute inaccurate items through the bureaus.** Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act you may dispute information you believe is inaccurate, and the furnisher must investigate and either verify or remove it. Items that cannot be verified come off. This is the mechanism that moved the file above, and it works because some reported information genuinely is wrong or unverifiable.

What this is not: disputing accurate derogatory information in the hope it disappears. That is not a strategy, and lenders can see dispute flags on a report, which can itself create an underwriting condition.

**Pay down revolving balances.** The fastest legitimate lever most people have. Utilization is a significant scoring factor, and it recalculates when balances report. Paying a card from near its limit down substantially can move a score in one reporting cycle, which is weeks, not months.

One specific note: **utilization is calculated per card and overall.** Paying one card to zero while another sits at its limit does less than spreading the paydown. Ask your lender to run a rapid rescore analysis if it matters for a tier.

**Do not close old accounts.** Closing a longstanding card removes available credit, raises utilization, and over time shortens average account age. The instinct to tidy up by closing unused cards is backwards.

**Do not open anything new.** New accounts add inquiries, lower average age, and on a file under review can reprice or break a loan outright.

**Bring everything current and keep it current.** Payment history is the largest factor. A single late payment reported during your escrow is genuinely damaging.

**Address collections carefully, with advice.** Paying an old collection does not automatically help and can occasionally reset recency depending on the scoring model in use. Which collections to address, in what order, and whether to negotiate is a conversation to have with your lender before you start writing checks.

## The timeline, honestly

**Rapid rescore after paying down balances:** days to a few weeks, and it requires documentation of the paydown. This is the only genuinely fast lever and it is limited to correcting reported balances, not removing items.

**Bureau disputes:** investigation periods run in weeks, and a result may require a second round. Budget a couple of months to do this properly.

**Rebuilding after a derogatory event:** months to years, depending on the event and the program's seasoning requirements.

**The practical conclusion:** if your score is near a threshold, start this **before** you shop, not after you are in contract. Sixty to ninety days of runway is the difference between having options and taking what is left.

## What to do right now if you are planning a purchase

1. **Pull your credit through a lender**, with a real analysis rather than a consumer app score, which frequently uses a different model than mortgage lending does.
2. **Ask which tier you are in and how far the next one is.** Get the distance in points.
3. **Ask what that tier is worth** in pricing, down payment, reserves, and program eligibility. Sometimes it is modest. Sometimes it is a different loan entirely.
4. **Get a specific plan**, naming which balances to pay and in what order, and which items are worth disputing.
5. **Then stop touching anything.** No new accounts, no closed accounts, no balance transfers, no cosigning for anyone.
6. **Then shop for the house.**

## If your income is also complicated

Credit work and income structure interact, and that is the real lesson of the file above. A borrower whose returns do not support the purchase has alternatives: [bank statement](/blog/bank-statement-loans-how-income-is-calculated/), [asset depletion](/blog/asset-depletion-asset-qualifier-loans/), or no-ratio financing. Several of those lean harder on credit precisely because they lean less on income.

Which means **credit work is worth more to a self-employed borrower than to a salaried one.** It is buying access to the programs that solve the income problem.

Get the credit analysis and the income analysis done together, before you shop. See [Pre-Qualified, Pre-Approved, Underwritten](/blog/underwritten-preapproval-vs-prequalification/).
