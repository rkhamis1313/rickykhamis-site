The case for staying in Old Town rather than at a North Scottsdale resort is simple: you can leave the car keys at the front desk. Old Town packs its dining, nightlife and culture into a few walkable blocks, and the hotels sit inside that footprint rather than twenty minutes from it.

Here are the stays worth knowing, and what each one is actually for.

## Hotel Valley Ho

The landmark. Open [since 1956](https://hotelvalleyho.com/), mid-century modern down to the bones, and genuinely part of Scottsdale's history rather than a theme applied to it. Resort-style grounds, and a short walk from the dining and nightlife.

If you care about design or want the version of Scottsdale that existed before the resorts got big, this is the one.

## Senna House, Curio Collection by Hilton

Modern, in the heart of Old Town, with a rooftop pool and a rooftop bar. On-site food and drink includes CALA and the Sonora Swim Club. Good for a group that wants to be in the middle of everything and does not want to walk far to start the evening.

## Canopy by Hilton Scottsdale Old Town

Upscale, with dark earthy rooms, a restaurant and a bar. The reason people book it is often on the roof: **Outrider Rooftop Lounge** sits on top and is one of the most talked-about rooftop bars in Scottsdale. Staying in the building is a reasonable shortcut to getting a spot at sunset.

## W Scottsdale

Right in the Entertainment District, with Cottontail Lounge on the second floor and the WET Deck pool as its centerpiece. This is the loud, in-the-middle-of-it option, and it is entirely deliberate. If you want to be where the night is, be here. If you want to sleep at midnight, do not.

## Scottsdale Marriott Suites Old Town

The practical choice. Suites, quieter positioning, and still inside the walkable core.

## How to choose

**Design and history:** Hotel Valley Ho.
**Rooftop and central:** Senna House or Canopy.
**In the middle of the nightlife:** W Scottsdale.
**Space and quiet, still walkable:** Marriott Suites.

**Season matters more than any of this.** October through April is peak, and spring training, the WM Phoenix Open and Barrett-Jackson each compress availability further. Summer is a different market entirely, in price and in atmosphere.

## Why a lender wrote a hotel guide

Because the hotel question and the financing question are closer than they look, and this is where Old Town buyers get caught.

**Resort-adjacent residential can be classified as a condotel, and condotels fall outside agency financing entirely.** A condominium project can be classified that way when it operates like a hotel: a front desk or registration area, daily housekeeping, short-term rental desk services, mandatory rental pooling, room-style keying, or central reservation systems. Some Old Town product sits close to that line, and a few sit over it.

The borrower never sees it coming, because it has nothing to do with the borrower. Ask directly and early whether a building offers hotel-style services or participates in a rental pool. See [Scottsdale Luxury Condo Financing](/blog/scottsdale-luxury-condo-project-approval/) for the full project review and the documents that answer it.

**The second trap is occupancy classification.** A second home is generally required to be under your exclusive control and not subject to a rental or management agreement that dictates when it is occupied. Hand that control to a management company and you have not got a second home any more, whatever the paperwork calls it. That changes your down payment and your pricing. See [Second Home or Investment Property in Scottsdale](/blog/scottsdale-second-home-vs-investment/).

**And the income you are counting on is discounted hard.** Old Town nightly income is seasonal and event-driven. Lenders underwrite a twelve month average, not your best six weeks, and most will not use projected nightly income at all. See [Spring Training, the Open, and Your Loan](/blog/scottsdale-short-term-rental-income-loan/).

The pattern across all three: **the building's operating model decides your financing before your credit ever enters the conversation.**

## If you are staying here to decide whether to buy here

Do this while you are in town. Walk the specific block at 11pm on a Friday, not just on a weekday afternoon. Ask the front desk what the building next door is. And get the association's rental policy and the project's owner-occupancy percentage before you write anything.
