The most common reason a veteran does not use the benefit they earned is that somebody told them they were not eligible. Frequently that somebody was wrong.

Eligibility is not one rule. It is a set of rules that differ by when you served, which component you served in, and how your service ended.

## The categories

[[RULE:va-eligibility-service]]

**Active duty service members.** Generally eligible after a minimum period of continuous service, and you do not have to wait until you separate. Plenty of people buy while still serving.

**Veterans.** Requirements vary by the era in which you served, and the minimum periods are different for wartime and peacetime service. This is where most of the wrong answers come from, because somebody applies the wrong era's rule.

**National Guard and Reserve.** Eligibility exists and the path is different from active duty, generally turning on years of service or a qualifying period of activation. A Guard member told flatly that the benefit is for active duty only has been told something incorrect.

**Surviving spouses.** A separate route with its own requirements. See [The Surviving Spouse Benefit](/blog/va-surviving-spouse-home-loan/).

[[RULE:va-surviving-spouse]]

## Discharge character matters

The character of your discharge affects eligibility, and this is worth raising directly rather than assuming the worst. A discharge that was less than fully honorable does not automatically end the question. Discharge upgrades exist, and determinations can be made on a case by case basis.

If you have been avoiding this conversation because of how your service ended, have it. The answer may not be what you expect.

## The document that settles it

[[RULE:va-certificate-of-eligibility]]

Your Certificate of Eligibility is the VA's own determination, and it ends the argument. A lender can pull it, usually quickly, and it tells you not just whether you are eligible but **how much entitlement you have available**, which is the number that actually matters if you have used the benefit before.

Have your DD-214 handy if you have separated, or a statement of service if you are still serving. Guard and Reserve members may need a points statement and evidence of service.

## What eligibility is not

**It is not a credit approval.** You can be fully eligible and still not qualify for a specific loan. Eligibility and underwriting are separate gates.

**It does not expire.** The benefit does not lapse because you did not use it in your twenties.

**It is not one use only.** Entitlement is restored when a prior VA loan is paid off, and some veterans carry two at once. See [Using Your VA Benefit Again](/blog/va-reuse-entitlement-second-va-loan/).

[[RULE:va-entitlement-restoration]]

**It is not limited to first-time buyers**, and it is not limited to single-family homes. The program finances up to four units if you live in one. See [The Fourplex Play](/blog/va-fourplex-house-hacking/).

## If you have been told no

Ask which rule they applied. A specific answer, naming your component and your service era, is a real answer. "VA loans are hard" is not.

Then get the Certificate of Eligibility and settle it with the VA's own determination rather than an originator's recollection.
