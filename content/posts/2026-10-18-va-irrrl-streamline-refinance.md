If you already have a VA loan and rates have fallen, the IRRRL is the cheapest, fastest way to capture that. It is also the transaction most often sold to veterans who should not do it.

## What it is

[[RULE:va-irrrl]]

The Interest Rate Reduction Refinance Loan refinances an existing VA loan into a new VA loan at a lower rate, with substantially reduced documentation. Typically **no new appraisal, no income verification and no new Certificate of Eligibility**, because the VA already guaranteed the loan being replaced.

That is why it is fast and cheap relative to a normal refinance. There is a funding fee, but it is markedly lower than on a purchase or a cash-out.

[[RULE:va-funding-fee]]

## The test that matters

An IRRRL has to produce a genuine benefit, and that is not a matter of opinion. There is a **recoupment** requirement: the costs of the refinance must be recovered by the monthly savings within a defined period, and there are requirements about the rate actually decreasing and about seasoning on the existing loan.

Those rules exist because veterans were being churned, refinanced repeatedly by lenders collecting fees on transactions that never paid for themselves.

So the question to ask is not "can I lower my rate." It is:

> *"What are my total costs, what is my monthly saving, and how many months until I am ahead?"*

If the answer is longer than you plan to keep the house, the IRRRL is not worth doing no matter how much lower the rate is.

**Be particularly careful about financing the costs.** Rolling them into the balance makes the monthly number look better while extending what you owe. That can still be the right call. It is not automatically the right call.

**And watch the term reset.** Refinancing a loan you are eight years into back out to thirty years lowers the payment and increases total interest substantially. If the goal is a lower rate rather than a lower payment, ask what a shorter term looks like.

## What an IRRRL will not do

**It will not get you cash.** An IRRRL is a rate reduction. If you need equity out, that is a VA cash-out refinance, which is a different transaction with full documentation and an appraisal.

[[RULE:va-cash-out-refinance]]

**It will not get you out of a VA loan.** You stay in the program. That is fine, because there is no monthly mortgage insurance to escape, which is exactly why a VA borrower has far less reason to leave their program than an FHA borrower does.

[[RULE:va-no-mortgage-insurance]]

Compare that with FHA, where above a certain loan-to-value the annual premium runs the life of the loan and refinancing out is the only exit. A VA borrower does not have that problem. See [How Long You Pay FHA Mortgage Insurance](/blog/fha-mortgage-insurance-how-long/).

**It will not generally let you remove a borrower** without care. Changing who is on the loan interacts with entitlement and occupancy, and is worth raising specifically rather than assuming.

## If you are getting cold-called about one

Veterans receive a lot of IRRRL solicitation, some of it aggressive and some of it misleading. A few things that should make you slow down:

- A quoted payment with no accompanying statement of total costs.
- Pressure about an offer expiring.
- Mail designed to look like it came from the VA or a government agency. **The VA does not make loans and does not solicit refinances.**
- A proposal that resets you to a fresh thirty year term without mentioning it.

Ask for a Loan Estimate and work out the recoupment yourself. If the person will not produce one, you have your answer.

## What to do

- **Know your current rate, balance, remaining term and whether you have mortgage insurance.** You do not, which is worth remembering when somebody compares you to an FHA borrower.
- **Get the total costs in writing**, not just the new payment.
- **Divide costs by monthly saving.** That is your breakeven in months. Compare it honestly to how long you will keep the house.
- **Ask what the same rate looks like on a shorter term.**
- **If you need cash rather than a lower rate, price the cash-out separately** and do not let the two be blended into one conversation.
