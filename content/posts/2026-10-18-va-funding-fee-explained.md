The VA loan has no down payment and no monthly mortgage insurance. It is not free, and the funding fee is where the cost sits.

It is also where a meaningful number of veterans overpay, because an exemption they qualified for was never applied.

## What it is

[[RULE:va-funding-fee]]

A one-time fee paid to the VA, which funds the guarantee that makes the whole program possible. It is generally expressed as a percentage of the loan amount, and the percentage depends on your situation rather than being one number for everybody.

What moves it:

- **Whether this is your first use of the benefit or a subsequent use.** Later uses generally cost more.
- **Whether you make a down payment.** Putting money down reduces the fee, which is one of the few reasons a VA borrower might choose to.
- **The type of transaction.** A purchase, a cash-out refinance and an IRRRL are treated differently, and the IRRRL fee is substantially lower.

**It can be financed into the loan** rather than paid in cash, which is what most borrowers do. That keeps the zero-down structure intact and means the fee shows up as a slightly larger balance rather than money at the closing table.

## The exemption

This is the part to read twice.

**A veteran receiving VA compensation for a service-connected disability is generally exempt from the funding fee entirely.** Not reduced. Exempt.

Also generally exempt: certain surviving spouses, and veterans who would be entitled to compensation but for receiving retirement or active duty pay.

On a typical Arizona purchase that is thousands of dollars that simply are not charged.

**Why it gets missed.** The exemption has to be identified and documented. Your Certificate of Eligibility generally reflects exempt status, but a file processed quickly by someone who has not done many VA loans can run straight past it. I have seen it happen.

Three situations worth raising directly:

**You have a rating but have never mentioned it.** Say so on the first call.

**You have a claim pending.** This matters. If a rating is awarded that is retroactively effective, a fee you already paid may be refundable. Do not assume a pending claim is irrelevant to your closing.

**You are a surviving spouse.** See [The Surviving Spouse Benefit](/blog/va-surviving-spouse-home-loan/).

## Put it in context before you flinch at it

The fee is a real cost and it is worth comparing honestly against the alternative rather than in isolation.

An FHA borrower pays an upfront premium **and** an annual premium that, above a certain loan-to-value, runs for the life of the loan. A conventional borrower under twenty percent down pays mortgage insurance monthly until it can be cancelled.

A VA borrower pays the funding fee once and then pays **no monthly mortgage insurance at all, ever**.

[[RULE:va-no-mortgage-insurance]]

Over any meaningful holding period that is not a close comparison. See [VA versus FHA versus Conventional](/blog/va-vs-fha-conventional/).

## What to do

- **Disclose any disability rating or pending claim on the first call.**
- **Check your Certificate of Eligibility for exempt status**, and ask your lender to confirm they have read it.
- **Ask whether financing the fee or paying it in cash is better for your situation.** Financing preserves cash; paying it keeps the balance down.
- **Ask whether a down payment would reduce the fee enough to be worth making**, which occasionally it is.
- **If you paid a fee and a rating was later awarded retroactively, ask about a refund.** Nobody will raise this for you.
