Scottsdale's golf reputation was built thirty minutes north, at Troon North, Grayhawk and Desert Mountain. But if you are staying in Old Town without a car, you are not out of options. There is a course about a mile away, and the oldest course in the city is barely further.

Here is what is genuinely close.

## Walking distance, or near enough

**[Continental Golf Club](https://golfscottsdale.org/blog/golf-courses-central-scottsdale)**, 7920 E. Osborn Road. An executive par 60 short course roughly 1.1 miles from Old Town, close enough to walk from most Old Town hotels. Not a championship test and not trying to be. It is the course you play when you have three hours and no car, and for that it is perfect.

**Coronado Golf Course**, 2829 N. Miller Road. Nine holes, parkland style, about 1.3 miles out, and it holds a title nothing else can claim: **the oldest golf course in Scottsdale.** Worth playing for that alone.

## A short ride

**Camelback Golf Club**, 7847 N. Mockingbird Lane. Two eighteen hole courses, The Padre and The Indian Bend, sitting at the base of Camelback Mountain. Resort golf, and a genuine step up in scale from the two above.

**McCormick Ranch**, about 3.7 miles out. The Pine and Palm courses, parkland style with mature trees, water features and Camelback Mountain views. This is the classic central Scottsdale golf experience and it is a very easy trip from Old Town.

**Talking Stick** and **Papago** are both within about fifteen minutes, and a rideshare from Old Town to Talking Stick typically runs in the ten to fifteen dollar range.

## How to plan it

**Play early.** October through April the tee sheets fill and the afternoons get busy. In summer, early is not a preference, it is the only sensible option.

**Book ahead in season.** Spring training, the WM Phoenix Open and Barrett-Jackson all compress tee times along with everything else in this city.

**If you only have a morning**, Continental. If you want the story, Coronado. If you want a real round, McCormick Ranch or Camelback.

## Where the golf question becomes a lending question

If you are visiting Old Town and thinking about buying into one of the golf communities you keep hearing about, there is one thing worth knowing before you go look, because it catches strong buyers repeatedly.

**Mandatory club dues are counted against your debt-to-income ratio like a car payment. A club initiation deposit is cash out of your accounts and generally does not count toward your reserve requirement.**

That combination is the single most common reason a Scottsdale golf community buyer gets a smaller approval than they expected. The money is real. It is just not where the guideline needs it to be, and if you are qualifying on assets it is worse, because the deposit reduces the very balance the calculation runs on.

The first question to answer at any address is whether membership is **mandatory or optional**, because an obligation you can decline generally is not counted and one you cannot is. Get the club's current membership plan and the community's governing documents in writing. Do not rely on what a neighbor told you or what was true three years ago.
