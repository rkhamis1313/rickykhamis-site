You applied, you waited, and the answer was no. Nobody explained it properly, so you are left assuming the problem is you.

Usually it is not. A mortgage decline comes from one of six specific places, and three of them have nothing to do with your credit or your income at all. Until you know which one you hit, you cannot fix it, and you are at real risk of fixing the wrong thing.

Here is the full list, and then how to find out which one applied to you.

## 1. Credit, and usually the overlay rather than the rule

The published minimum for a loan program and the minimum your lender actually uses are two different numbers. The second one is called an overlay, every lender has them, they vary enormously between investors, and they are not published anywhere.

So the most common version of this decline is not "your credit is too low for this loan." It is "your credit is too low for **this lender's** version of this loan." Those are completely different problems with completely different solutions. See [What Credit Score FHA Actually Requires](/blog/fha-credit-score-arizona/).

Other credit-side reasons:

- **A recent significant event**, where the waiting period has not run. The clock usually starts at the completion date rather than the filing date, which catches people out in both directions. See [Getting an FHA Loan After a Bankruptcy or Foreclosure](/blog/fha-after-bankruptcy-arizona/).
- **Housing payment lates**, which get weighted more heavily than almost anything else.
- **Thin or no credit file.** Living without debt on purpose is a virtue that underwriting struggles to score.
- **Unresolved collections or judgments**, where a recorded judgment is also a title problem. See [Collections, Judgments and Federal Debt](/blog/fha-collections-judgments-federal-debt/).

## 2. Debt-to-income

Your total monthly obligations against your qualifying income. This is where most approvals are genuinely won or lost, and the reason it surprises people is that the numbers an underwriter uses frequently are not the numbers you would use.

**Your income may be lower than you think.** If you are self-employed, qualifying income is rebuilt from filed returns with add-backs and exclusions, and a declining second year generally becomes your figure rather than the average. Business owners are routinely shocked by the result. See [Business Owner Write-Offs and Your Mortgage](/blog/business-owner-write-offs-mortgage/).

**Your debts may be higher than you think.** A deferred student loan can be counted at a figure far above what you actually pay. Support you pay counts. A co-signed loan for a relative counts even though you never make the payment. See [How FHA Counts Your Student Loans](/blog/fha-student-loans-dti/).

## 3. Assets that cannot be sourced

You have the money and the loan still fails, because having it is not the test. **Documenting where it came from** is the test.

- **Cash deposits cannot be sourced.** There is no paper trail, so on most programs the money cannot be used at all.
- **A gift without documentation** is not usable. The donor's bank statement is the step that stalls these, because some donors refuse. See [Using Gift Funds for an FHA Down Payment](/blog/fha-gift-funds-down-payment/).
- **Any large deposit will be questioned.** Not as an accusation. As a requirement. See [Large Deposits and NSF Items](/blog/large-deposits-nsf-bank-statements/).
- **Reserves.** The quiet one. Reserves are the liquid assets you must still hold **after** closing, and a borrower who puts every dollar into the down payment can clear the down payment and fail the reserve test. This decline feels completely arbitrary if nobody warned you.

## 4. The property, not you

This is the category almost nobody expects, and it accounts for a large share of declines on files where the borrower was perfectly qualified.

- **The appraisal came in under the contract price**, so the loan resizes and the gap has to be covered in cash. See [When the Appraisal Comes In Low](/blog/appraisal-gap-arizona-contract/).
- **The condo project failed review.** Owner occupancy ratio, reserve funding, insurance, litigation, or one owner holding too many units. You can be flawless and be declined over a building you do not control. See [Does the Condo Have to Be FHA Approved?](/blog/fha-condo-approval-arizona/).
- **The appraiser called out condition items** that have to be repaired before funding. See [What an FHA Appraiser Will Make the Seller Fix](/blog/fha-appraisal-repairs-sellers/).
- **Water or septic could not be documented.** On desert acreage this is routine and it is a real reason some investors simply will not lend. See [Well Water and Mortgage Financing in Arizona](/blog/well-water-mortgage-arizona/).
- **Title problems.** A lien, an easement, a probate matter, a name mismatch in the chain. These need weeks.

If your decline was in this category, **nothing about you needs to change.** That is worth knowing before you spend six months on your credit for no reason.

## 5. Employment and income stability

Not the amount. The durability.

- **A job change mid-process.** Employment is re-verified before closing, and a change discovered then can stop a closing cold.
- **A gap in history**, which is a question rather than a disqualification, and needs an explanation.
- **Income that is ending.** Support with a near termination date, a contract with a short remaining term, or a bonus with no history.
- **Self-employment under two years**, where the default answer is wait and the exception for someone who did the same work as an employee first is real and routinely missed. See [The Same-Industry Exception](/blog/fha-self-employed-arizona/).

## 6. Something in a screening you never saw

The ambush category.

Government-backed files are screened against a federal database of delinquent federal debt. **A defaulted federal student loan from years ago, long since off your credit report, can still show there and make you ineligible** with a clean report and a good score. Nobody anticipates this. See [Collections, Judgments and Federal Debt](/blog/fha-collections-judgments-federal-debt/).

## How to find out which one you hit

Do not guess, and do not accept a vague verbal explanation. You have a right to better than that.

**Ask for the adverse action notice.** When a lender takes adverse action on a credit application, federal law generally entitles you to a statement of the specific reasons for the decision, or notice of your right to request that statement. Separately, where the decision was based on information in a credit report, there are disclosure obligations about that too, including information about the score used.

Most declined borrowers never ask, and so they never learn what actually happened. Ask, in writing, and get the specific reasons rather than a category.

That notice converts a vague no into a diagnosis, which is the whole point. See [What to Do After a Mortgage Denial](/blog/after-a-mortgage-denial/).

## The question to ask before you change anything

> *"Was I declined because of the guideline, or because of your overlay?"*

If the answer is the overlay, **you may already qualify somewhere else today**, with nothing about your file changing. That is not a loophole, it is how a market with many investors works, and it is the single most useful thing a declined borrower can understand. See [Declined by One Lender, Approved by Another](/blog/declined-by-one-lender-approved-by-another/).

If the answer is the guideline, then you have real work to do, and it is worth doing in the right order. Credit work done before a file is structured changes which programs exist for you. The same work attempted after an offer is accepted changes nothing, because the calendar will not allow it. See [Fix the Credit First](/blog/credit-repair-before-mortgage/).
