*Part of the [VA loans in Arizona](/loan-programs/va-loans/) guide, where every rule on this page is cited and dated.*

The VA loan is the strongest financing available to most people who can get it, and it is routinely treated as a fallback. Veterans get steered to conventional loans by agents who think VA deals are hard, and to FHA by originators who have never closed one.

Here is what the program actually does, question by question, with the rule and the overlay kept separate. The VA sets a floor. Your lender decides what they will actually do. Those are different numbers.

## What the VA actually is

The VA does not lend you money. The Department of Veterans Affairs **guarantees** a portion of a loan made by a private lender, and that guarantee is what lets the lender do things it otherwise would not: lend with nothing down, charge no monthly mortgage insurance, and accept a borrower with no published minimum credit score.

Which means, as with FHA, that the lender still carries risk the guarantee does not cover, and so lenders layer their own requirements on top. Those are overlays. They are legal, universal, unpublished, and they vary enormously.

## The four things that make it the best loan available

**No down payment.**

[[RULE:va-no-down-payment]]

**No monthly mortgage insurance.** Not reduced. None. Over a long hold this is worth more than most borrowers realize, and it is the clearest advantage over FHA, where the premium frequently runs the life of the loan.

[[RULE:va-no-mortgage-insurance]]

**No VA minimum credit score.** The VA publishes none. Every lender sets their own, and they differ. A veteran declined at one bank may be approvable at another with nothing about their file changing. See [There Is No VA Credit Score Minimum](/blog/va-no-credit-score-minimum/).

[[RULE:va-no-minimum-credit-score]]

**The loan is assumable.** Somebody can take over your note, at your rate. In a market where rates have risen, a below-market assumable loan is a real asset when you sell, and almost no seller knows they are holding one.

[[RULE:va-assumability]]

See [Your VA Loan Is Assumable](/blog/va-assumable-loan-advantage/).

## The cost: the funding fee

[[RULE:va-funding-fee]]

The part people miss: **a service-connected disability rating may exempt you entirely.** That exemption is worth real money and it is routinely not caught. If you have a rating, or a claim pending, say so on the first call. See [The Funding Fee](/blog/va-funding-fee-explained/).

## How VA underwrites differently from everyone else

This is the part that makes VA genuinely distinct, and most originators cannot explain it.

[[RULE:va-residual-income]]

Residual income is what is actually left over each month after the mortgage, the debts, taxes, utilities and maintenance, measured against a requirement that scales with **your family size and your region**. No other loan program tests this.

The practical effect: a veteran who fails a pure debt-to-income test can still be approved on strong residual income. It is the reason a VA file sometimes works when a conventional file on identical numbers does not.

[[RULE:va-dti-compensating]]

See [Residual Income Explained](/blog/va-residual-income-explained/).

## The strategy nobody tells veterans about

The VA loan finances up to four units with nothing down, as long as you live in one of them.

[[RULE:va-multi-unit]]

[[RULE:va-rental-income-multiunit]]

Live in one, rent three, let the tenants carry most of the payment, refinance out after a couple of years to restore your entitlement, and do it again. Four properties is sixteen doors inside a decade.

It is real, it is written into the program, and it requires you to move four times. See [The Fourplex Play](/blog/va-fourplex-house-hacking/) for the full math and an honest account of the four things that break it.

## Should you use VA at all?

Almost always, if you are eligible. The exceptions are narrow: a buyer putting down enough to avoid mortgage insurance anyway and wanting to preserve entitlement for later, or a property type VA will not finance.

See [VA versus FHA versus Conventional](/blog/va-vs-fha-conventional/).

## What to do first

- **Get your Certificate of Eligibility**, or let a lender pull it.
- **Ask about the funding fee exemption** if you have any disability rating or pending claim.
- **Ask your lender for their credit overlay**, in writing. Not the VA minimum, theirs.
- **Ask whether they have actually closed VA loans**, and how many. This program rewards experience and punishes guessing.
- **If you are buying multi-unit, say so on the first call**, because the rental income analysis changes the whole file.
