A listing with multiple reductions and no offers is a specific diagnosis, not a general one. And the answer is almost never a seventh reduction.

Here is how to read it and what to do instead.

## What repeated reductions actually communicate

Buyers and their agents read price history. A listing with a long reduction trail tells them three things, none of which you intended:

**Something is wrong with it.** Buyers assume the market has already evaluated this house and declined. They do not know why, so they invent a reason, and the reason they invent is worse than the truth.

**Wait for the next one.** A seller who has cut six times has established a pattern. The rational buyer response is to wait rather than write, because the pattern predicts another cut. Every reduction after the second one actively trains buyers to wait.

**The seller is motivated.** Which sounds useful and is not. It invites offers well below the current ask rather than at it.

The reductions are no longer functioning as price discovery. They are functioning as a reason not to write.

## Diagnose before you restructure

Be honest here, because the wrong diagnosis wastes the seller's money a seventh time.

**Are you getting showings?**

*No showings at all* is a pricing or presentation problem. Pull the comparables again, coldly. If similar homes are closing well below your current ask, you are still high and a buydown will not fix it. Also look hard at the photography and the first image, because a large share of buyers never get past it.

*Showings but no offers* is the situation this post is about. Buyers are coming, looking, and declining. The house is acceptable at the price or they would not tour it. Something at the conversion step is failing, and in this market it is usually the payment.

*Offers that fall apart* is an inspection, appraisal or financing problem. Different post, different fix.

**What is the competition doing?** If a builder community within a few miles is advertising a rate in the fives while you advertise a price, you are not in the same contest as far as the buyer is concerned. See [Why Builders Advertise Rates](/blog/why-builders-advertise-low-rates/).

**How long has it been since anything about the listing changed except the number?** A reduction is not new information to a buyer who already saw and passed. New photography, a new first image, and a new headline are.

## The restructure

If the diagnosis is showings without offers, stop cutting and change the category of the offer.

**1. Hold the price.** Another reduction reinforces the wait-for-the-next-one pattern. Breaking that pattern requires doing something other than what you have been doing.

**2. Take the dollars from the reduction you were about to make and point them at the rate.** Run the comparison first so the seller can see it rather than take your word for it. See [Seller Concession vs Price Reduction](/blog/seller-concession-vs-price-reduction-math/).

**3. Lock the rate to the address** so the number you advertise is real and defensible rather than an estimate that may not survive the week. That is the whole purpose of [List & Lock](https://www.epiqlending.com/mysite/Ricky-Khamis/listnlock), and on a stale listing it does something extra: because buyers qualify on the locked rate, it can make approvable the buyers who toured, loved it, and could not get a lender to say yes.

**4. Relaunch the marketing properly.** New photos or at least a new lead image, new public remarks that open with the payment, a sign rider, and a flyer with the payment ladder. A concession buried in agent remarks produces nothing. See [How Listing Agents Should Market the Payment](/blog/listing-agent-payment-marketing-playbook/).

**5. Go back to everyone who toured and did not write.** This is the step that gets skipped and it is frequently where the offer comes from. Those buyers liked the house enough to spend a Saturday on it. The reason they did not write may be exactly the one you just fixed. Call their agents with a specific number, not a general announcement.

## The conversation with the seller

Sellers who have reduced six times are tired and defensive, and they expect the seventh conversation to be another request to cut. Walk in with something else.

> I am not going to ask you to reduce again. The last three did not produce an offer and I do not think a fourth will. What I want to show you is what the same dollars do if we point them at the buyer's payment instead of the price.

Then hand them the comparison. Current price with today's payment. The reduction they were braced for, with the payment it produces. The same dollars as a buydown, with the first year payment. The year four payment stated plainly so they know you are not selling them something fragile.

Most sellers have never seen those four numbers next to each other. The sheet does the persuading.

## One honest caveat

If the comparables genuinely say the house is overpriced, say so and reduce. A buydown on an overpriced listing produces an overpriced listing with a buydown, and you will have spent the seller's money and their patience to arrive back at the same conversation.

This is a tool for correctly priced houses that are losing a payment comparison. That is a large share of stale inventory in this market. It is not all of it.
