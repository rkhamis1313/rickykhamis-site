Rio Verde sits northeast of Scottsdale past the 101, desert and quiet with the Mazatzals on the horizon. It draws second home buyers for obvious reasons: space, views, golf, and a drive to north Scottsdale that is shorter than the isolation suggests.

Before you talk about rates, talk about water. In this specific area that is not a lifestyle question, it is a financing question, and it is the one that catches buyers from out of state.

## Rio Verde and Rio Verde Foothills are different

They sound like the same place and they are not.

**Rio Verde** proper, including the community at Rio Verde and the Trilogy at Verde River development, is a developed community with organized water service.

**Rio Verde Foothills** is unincorporated county land, parcel by parcel, with no single water provider. Many parcels there are on private wells. A meaningful number historically depended on **hauled water**, delivered by truck to a storage tank, and that arrangement became a public problem when the City of Scottsdale stopped supplying haulers serving the area, leaving households scrambling for a source. Arrangements have shifted since, including a standpipe district formed to serve the area, but the underlying point for a buyer is permanent: **water service in the Foothills is parcel-specific and must be verified in writing for the exact address.**

A lender's position on this is not complicated. The collateral needs a sufficient, potable, legally available water supply. A parcel whose water arrangement cannot be documented to an investor's satisfaction is a parcel some investors will decline, regardless of how strong the borrower is.

## What to verify, in order, before you write

**1. Which water situation the exact parcel is in.** Not the area. The parcel. Municipal or community service, a private well, a shared well, or a hauled water arrangement with a storage tank.

**2. If it is a well, the documentation.** Well registration, potability results, and a flow or yield test where the program requires one. Get the well's recent history if you can.

**3. If it is a shared well, the recorded agreement.** Investors have views on what a shared well agreement must contain, and not every agreement satisfies every investor.

**4. If water is hauled, understand this clearly:** financing options narrow substantially and some investors will not lend at all. This is the item to raise on your very first lender call, before you fall in love with a property. A cash purchase is sometimes the only path, and that is a decision to make knowingly rather than discover in week three.

**5. The septic system**, since most parcels out here are on one, including the Arizona transfer of ownership inspection. See [The Arizona Septic Transfer Inspection](/blog/arizona-septic-transfer-inspection-loan/).

**6. Insurance availability and cost**, which for remote desert property with distance to a fire station is a real line item and sometimes a surprising one.

## What second home occupancy adds

Separate from the property, buying this as a second home rather than a primary residence changes your loan.

**Pricing and down payment are different.** Second home financing generally carries higher pricing and higher minimum down payment than a primary residence, and the gap widens on a jumbo balance.

**Reserves are tested harder.** Expect to document liquid reserves after closing, frequently measured in months of payments on both this property and your primary residence.

**Occupancy has to be credible.** An underwriter looks at distance from your primary residence, whether the property is a plausible personal use second home, and whether anything in the file suggests it is really an investment. Which it may be, and that is fine, but it has to be financed as what it is. Misrepresenting occupancy is loan fraud, not a technicality. See [Second Home vs Investment Property](/blog/scottsdale-second-home-vs-investment/).

**Rental intent changes everything.** If you plan to rent it at all, say so up front. Short term rental use puts you in a different financing category with different programs, and the right structure may be a DSCR loan sized on the property's income rather than yours. See [Short-Term Rental DSCR Loans](/blog/short-term-rental-dscr-scottsdale/) and [Scottsdale Short-Term Rental Income Loans](/blog/scottsdale-short-term-rental-income-loan/).

**Concession room is tighter than on a primary residence.** If you are negotiating seller help, the cap on a second home is generally more restrictive. See [Seller Concession Limits by Loan Type](/blog/seller-concession-limits-by-loan-type/).

## The appraisal out here

Low transaction volume, large and varied parcels, and custom homes built across decades. That combination stretches the comparable sales set and grows the adjustments, which means appraisal risk on a file with a closing date attached.

Order it early. Decide in advance what you do with a short value, and if you are competing, understand how an appraisal contingency interacts with your offer before you waive anything. See [Appraisal Gaps on an Arizona Contract](/blog/appraisal-gap-arizona-contract/).

## Before you write in Rio Verde

- **Get the water situation for the exact parcel in writing.** Everything else is secondary.
- **If water is hauled, call a lender before you make an offer.** Not after.
- **Get the septic transfer inspection on the calendar** at contract.
- **Price insurance early**, including the distance-to-fire-station question.
- **Confirm your occupancy classification honestly** and finance it as what it is.
- **Document reserves for both properties** after closing.
- **Order the appraisal early** and plan for thin comparables.
