A veteran gets declined, is told their score is too low for a VA loan, and concludes the benefit is not available to them.

The VA did not decline them. A lender did, using a number the VA never published.

## What the VA actually requires

[[RULE:va-no-minimum-credit-score]]

The VA's own approach is about the whole credit picture, not a threshold score. Recent payment history, whether past problems are resolved, and whether the borrower has re-established themselves all matter more than a single number.

## What lenders require

A number. Theirs. And it varies more on VA than on almost any other program, precisely because there is no published floor to anchor everybody to the same place.

Three things that follow:

**Every lender has one.** A lender saying they have no minimum has not told you theirs.

**They are far apart.** The spread between the most conservative bank and the most accommodating investor on a VA file is wide. That spread is your opportunity.

**They are not published.** You have to ask, and most veterans have never heard the word overlay.

## The question that sorts it

> *"Is that the VA's requirement, or your overlay?"*

The honest answer is always "ours," because the VA does not publish one. A lender who tells you the VA requires a specific score is either mistaken or not being straight with you, and either way you have learned something.

If the obstacle is an overlay, **you may be approvable somewhere else today with nothing about your file changing.** That is not a loophole. It is how a market with many investors works, and it is the strongest argument there is for using a broker on a credit-sensitive VA file.

See [Declined by One Lender, Approved by Another](/blog/declined-by-one-lender-approved-by-another/).

## Where residual income rescues VA files

This is the part that makes VA genuinely different and that most originators cannot explain.

[[RULE:va-residual-income]]

VA tests what is actually left in your pocket each month after the mortgage, the debts, estimated taxes, utilities and maintenance, against a requirement scaled to your family size and your region.

A veteran whose ratio looks tight can pass comfortably on residual income. A borrower who would be declined on a conventional debt-to-income test can be approved here. It is a genuinely different test and it exists because the program was designed around what a household actually needs to live on.

## What to do if your credit is the obstacle

Credit work done **before** a file is structured changes which programs are available. The same work after an offer is accepted changes nothing, because reporting cycles do not fit inside a thirty day escrow.

- **Pay down revolving balances.** Fastest legitimate lever, and it recalculates when balances report.
- **Dispute genuinely inaccurate items** through the bureaus. Weeks, sometimes two rounds.
- **Do not close old accounts**, which raises utilization and shortens average account age.
- **Do not open anything new.**
- **Ask before paying old collections**, because it does not automatically help.
- **Budget sixty to ninety days.**

See [Fix the Credit First](/blog/credit-repair-before-mortgage/).

## The thing I want you to take away

Nobody is entitled to a loan, and some files genuinely do not work yet. But the sentence "you do not qualify for a VA loan because of your credit score" is, as a statement about the VA, not true.

It is a statement about one lender. Find out which, and then decide whether to fix your file or change who is looking at it.
