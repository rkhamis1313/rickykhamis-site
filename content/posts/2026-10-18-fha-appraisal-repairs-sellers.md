Plenty of listing agents treat an FHA offer as a weaker offer, and a buyer with an FHA pre-approval loses contested properties partly because of it.

Some of that reputation is earned and some is folklore. Worth separating, because if you are a seller, understanding it protects your closing date, and if you are a buyer, understanding it makes your offer stronger.

## Why an FHA appraisal is different

An FHA appraisal does two jobs. It establishes value, like any appraisal. It also assesses whether the property meets **minimum property requirements**, because HUD is insuring the loan and will not insure collateral that is unsafe or unsound.

[[RULE:fha-appraisal-repairs]]

Two consequences:

**An appraiser can condition the appraisal on repairs.** If something fails the standard, the loan generally cannot close until it is corrected and documented. Now you have a repair negotiation in the middle of escrow with a closing date running.

**The appraiser works for the lender, not for you.** You cannot pressure them, and appraiser independence rules govern communication. Information can be provided through the lender; opinions cannot be pushed.

## What actually gets called out

In this market, on Arizona housing, the recurring items:

**Roof condition and remaining life.** The most common call-out by a wide margin. A roof at the end of its service life generates a condition. On flat or low-slope roofs, which a lot of mid-century Phoenix architecture has, this comes up constantly.

**Peeling or chipping paint on pre-1978 housing**, because of lead-based paint requirements. This surprises sellers more than anything else on the list, and on an older Phoenix or Scottsdale home it is frequently the only item standing between a file and a clear-to-close. It is also cheap to fix in advance and expensive to discover in week three.

**Exposed wiring, open junction boxes, missing cover plates.** Small, cheap, and a genuine call-out.

**Non-functioning mechanical systems.** Heating and cooling have to work. In Phoenix a non-functioning air conditioner is not a cosmetic issue.

**Plumbing leaks and non-functioning fixtures.**

**Missing handrails** on stairs, and trip or fall hazards.

**Broken windows, or windows that will not operate.**

**Pool condition.** Common here and worth attention. An empty, green, or non-functioning pool with failed equipment is a safety and habitability item. Pool fencing and barrier requirements also come into it.

**Water heater strapping and a functioning pressure relief configuration.**

**Standing water, grading that directs water at the foundation, evidence of active leaks.**

**Wood-destroying organism damage.** Termites are real in Arizona and evidence of active infestation or structural damage is a finding.

**Well and septic, where applicable.** On desert acreage this is its own project with its own calendar. See [Well Water and Mortgage Financing in Arizona](/blog/well-water-mortgage-arizona/) and [The Arizona Septic Transfer Inspection](/blog/arizona-septic-transfer-inspection-loan/).

## What does not get called out, contrary to the folklore

This is where the reputation outruns the reality. An FHA appraiser is generally **not** requiring:

- Cosmetic condition. Dated kitchens, worn carpet, unfashionable tile.
- Minor deferred maintenance that is not a safety, security or soundness issue.
- Code compliance for an older home built to an earlier standard.
- A perfect home inspection. The appraisal and the home inspection are different documents with different purposes, and a buyer's inspector finding something does not make it an FHA condition.

The standard is safety, security and structural soundness, not condition or taste. A tired but sound house generally passes.

## For listing agents: the play

You can take most of the risk out of an FHA offer before you ever receive one.

**Walk the property against the list above when you take the listing.** Paint, handrails, cover plates, the pool, a visibly failing roof. Most of it is inexpensive and the alternative is discovering it mid-escrow.

**Deal with pre-1978 peeling paint proactively.** Cheapest insurance on this list.

**Know the roof's age and condition before you market it.** If it is marginal, decide in advance what you will do. Discovering it at the appraisal with a closing date attached is the worst version of that conversation.

**Get the systems running.** If the air conditioning or the water heater is out, fix it before photos, not after an appraisal.

**Price the repairs rather than reject the buyer.** An FHA buyer with strong credit and real reserves is frequently a better closing risk than a conventional buyer who is stretched. The appraisal items are usually a few hundred to a few thousand dollars. Rejecting the offer outright on principle costs more than that.

**Understand the appraisal follows the property.** An FHA appraisal is tied to the property for a period, so if the deal falls apart, the value and conditions can follow it to the next FHA buyer. That is a real reason to resolve conditions rather than to walk and relist, and a real reason to care about getting the appraisal right the first time.

## For buyers: how to make the offer stronger

**Be underwritten, not pre-qualified.** The single biggest thing you can do about the perception of FHA offers. A listing agent comparing offers is pricing certainty of closing. See [Pre-Qualified, Pre-Approved, Underwritten](/blog/underwritten-preapproval-vs-prequalification/).

**Have your lender call the listing agent.** An originator who will speak to the file credibly is worth real money on a contested property.

**Choose your property with the list above in mind.** A visibly failing roof on a home where the seller has no money for repairs is a predictable problem. Know that before you fall in love with it.

**Do not waive an inspection to compete.** Particularly here. The appraisal is not an inspection and will not protect you.

**Ask about a renovation structure if the house needs real work.** A property that cannot pass as-is may still be financeable with the repairs included in the loan.

## The honest summary

FHA appraisals are stricter on property condition than conventional, and that is the actual reason behind the reputation. The strictness is about safety and soundness, not cosmetics, and most of what gets called out is cheap and predictable.

A seller who spends a weekend on paint, handrails and cover plates has removed most of the risk. A buyer who is fully underwritten and whose lender answers the phone has removed most of the rest.
