Buying Your First Home in Gilbert: New Build vs. Resale, and the Costs Nobody Quotes You

By Ricky Khamis · September 4, 2026 · 3 min read

Gilbert is a different buy than Scottsdale. More master-planned communities, more new construction competing directly with resale, more families stretching for a specific school boundary.

That changes the decision. In Gilbert, the question usually isn't just "can I qualify?" — it's "builder or resale?" Most first-time buyers answer that on emotion and sticker price. Here's how to answer it on math.

Compare the payment, never the price

A builder's advertised price and a resale asking price are not comparable numbers. Put both through the same filter before you decide anything.

Line itemNew buildResale
Base priceUsually firmerUsually more negotiable
Lender incentiveOften significant, but tied to the builder's lenderRare
Closing cost creditsCommon on standing inventoryNegotiable
Lot premiumReal money, often overlookedN/A
Landscaping, blinds, fencingFrequently not includedAlready done
AppliancesSometimes partialUsually included
HOA duesVaries by communityKnown and verifiable
Special taxing districtPossible in newer East Valley communitiesLess common
Property tax basisMay be assessed on land initially, then resetAlready stabilized

That last pair matters more than people expect.

Special taxing districts. Some newer Arizona master-planned communities sit inside a Community Facilities District, which funds infrastructure through an additional assessment on your tax bill. It is a legitimate structure — but it's a real, recurring cost that is not in the sales-office payment sheet. Ask directly: "Is this parcel inside a CFD or any special assessment district?" Get the answer in writing.

Property tax reset. On a new build, the first tax bill can reflect an unimproved lot. Once the home is assessed, your escrow payment can jump — sometimes materially — at the first escrow analysis. Budget for the assessed-home number, not the first bill you see.

The builder incentive question, answered honestly

Builders often offer meaningful incentives if you use their affiliated lender. Sometimes that's genuinely the best deal available. Sometimes it isn't.

You are allowed to shop. Federal rules prohibit a seller from requiring you to use a specific title or settlement provider as a condition of sale, and you always have the right to compare loan offers.

Here's the honest way to evaluate it:

  1. Get the builder lender's Loan Estimate. Not a flyer, not a payment sheet — the actual Loan Estimate form.
  2. Get a competing Loan Estimate on the same loan type, same price, same down payment, same lock period.
  3. Compare page 2 line by line: origination charges, discount points, and third-party fees.
  4. Then price the incentive. If the builder credit exceeds the total cost difference over the time you'll realistically hold the loan, take the incentive. If it doesn't, it's a discount on a more expensive loan.

That's it. It's arithmetic, and it takes one afternoon. Do it before you're emotionally committed to a lot number.

Don't rule yourself out of assistance

Same message as everywhere else in the Valley, and it applies fully in Gilbert: Home Plus is available statewide, in every county and zip code in Arizona.

  • It does not require first-time buyer status.
  • The 2026 borrower income limit was $155,386 — higher than most families assume.
  • Assistance comes as a forgivable second toward down payment and closing costs, forgiven provided you don't sell or refinance within the first 36 months.
  • One borrower must complete a homebuyer education course before closing.

Caps and terms move. Confirm the current guidelines rather than planning around a number you read once. But run the eligibility check — the cost of checking is an email, and the cost of assuming is thousands of dollars.

Before you sign a builder contract

New-construction contracts are written by the builder, for the builder. Read for these specifically:

  • Completion timeline and delay remedies. What happens to your rate lock if the home slips a quarter?
  • Rate lock length and extension cost. Long builds need long locks. Extensions aren't free.
  • Earnest money and design-center deposits. Which portions are refundable, and under what conditions?
  • Appraisal contingency. Does the contract protect you if the appraisal comes in under contract price?
  • Change-order pricing. Design-center upgrades are financed at your mortgage rate for 30 years. A $20,000 upgrade package is not a $20,000 decision.
  • Warranty terms and the walkthrough process. Get the punch-list procedure in writing.

If you're financing through a long build cycle, talk to your lender about lock strategy before you sign, not after. That single conversation is worth more than any upgrade in the design center.

The Gilbert sequence

  1. Get fully underwritten first — same as anywhere.
  2. Build a whole-payment comparison: price, HOA, taxes (assessed, not initial), and any special district.
  3. If it's new construction, get two Loan Estimates and do the arithmetic on the incentive.
  4. Check Home Plus eligibility instead of assuming you're over the line.
  5. Read the builder contract for timeline, lock, and appraisal protection before you sign.
  6. Freeze your credit and your accounts until you close.

Gilbert is a good place to buy a first home. It's a bad place to guess. Do the math on the full payment, and the decision usually makes itself.
