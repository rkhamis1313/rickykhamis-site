What to Do After a Mortgage Denial: Get the Letter, Then Work the Reason

By Ricky Khamis · October 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Verified and written by Ricky Khamis, President of EPiQ Lending, lending in Arizona since 1999. NMLS #173141 · EPiQ Lending NMLS #1936984 · Scottsdale, AZ. Every guideline on this page carries its source and the date it was verified.(480) 999-9842

The decline is not the expensive part. Guessing at why is the expensive part.

I have met borrowers who spent a year rebuilding credit when their file died on an appraisal, and borrowers who gave up entirely when the only obstacle was one lender's internal minimum. Both outcomes came from the same mistake: acting without a diagnosis.

Here is the sequence that gets you a diagnosis.

Step 1: get the adverse action notice, in writing

This is the step nearly everybody skips, and it is your legal right.

When a lender takes adverse action on a credit application, federal law under the Equal Credit Opportunity Act and Regulation B generally requires that you be given either a statement of the specific reasons for the decision or notice of your right to request that statement within a defined period. Separately, where the decision relied on information in a consumer report, the Fair Credit Reporting Act carries its own disclosure obligations, including telling you that a report was used and giving you information about the credit score relied on and how to obtain the report.

What that means practically:

You are entitled to specifics, not a shrug. "Insufficient credit" is a category. "Debt-to-income ratio exceeds program maximum" and "appraised value insufficient to support loan amount" are diagnoses, and they point at completely different work.

Ask in writing and keep the response. If you only got a phone call, request the notice. If what arrived is vague, ask for the specific reasons.

Request your credit report and the score they used. You cannot argue with information you have not read, and reports contain errors more often than people expect.

I am a lender, not a lawyer, and the exact notices, timeframes and rights depend on your facts. Confirm the specifics against the current regulations or with a qualified advisor. But ask. The notice is the single most valuable document in this process and it costs you nothing.

Step 2: sort the reason into one of four buckets

Every decline reason falls into one of these, and the bucket determines everything you do next.

Bucket A: it was the property, not you. The appraisal came in short, the condo project failed review, title had a problem, water or septic could not be documented, the appraiser called out repairs.

What this means: nothing about you needs to change. A different property can close on your existing approval. This is the best possible decline and it is routinely misread as a personal failure.

Bucket B: it was the overlay, not the guideline. The program permits your file; this lender's internal minimum does not.

What this means: you may be approvable somewhere else today, with no change at all. See Declined by One Lender, Approved by Another.

Bucket C: it was a documentation failure. The money was there and could not be sourced. The income exists and was not calculated correctly, or the right program was never tried. A gift arrived as cash. A deposit was never explained.

What this means: this is frequently fixable in weeks rather than years, and sometimes the fix is a different documentation path rather than a change to your finances. A self-employed borrower declined on tax returns may qualify on a bank statement analysis of the same business.

Bucket D: it was genuinely you, against the actual guideline. The score is below the program floor. The ratio does not work at any lender. The waiting period after a credit event has not run.

What this means: real work on a real timeline. Which is worth doing properly, and the rest of this page is mostly about that.

Ask your lender directly which bucket you are in. If they will not or cannot tell you, that itself is information.

Step 3: work the actual reason

If it was credit:

Sequence matters more than effort here. Credit work done before a file is structured changes which programs are available to you. The same work attempted after an offer is accepted changes nothing, because reporting cycles do not fit inside a thirty day escrow.

  • Pay down revolving balances. The fastest legitimate lever. Utilization recalculates when balances report, so this can move in one cycle. It is measured per card and overall, so spreading a paydown generally beats zeroing one and leaving another at its limit.
  • Dispute what is genuinely inaccurate through the bureaus. The furnisher must investigate and either verify or remove. Weeks, sometimes two rounds.
  • Do not close old accounts. It raises utilization and shortens average account age.
  • Do not open anything new.
  • Ask before paying old collections. It does not automatically help and can occasionally reset recency.
  • Budget sixty to ninety days.

See Fix the Credit First.

If it was the ratio: reduce monthly obligations rather than chasing income, because a cleared car payment frees ratio room immediately. Also ask whether a permanent rate buydown would close the gap, since it lowers the note rate and therefore the qualifying payment. A temporary buydown generally will not, because you are qualified at the note rate. See Permanent vs Temporary Buydown.

If it was income documentation: ask for a different analysis of the same income. Bank statement, profit and loss, asset depletion, or no-ratio structures each prove repayment capacity differently. A decline on tax returns is not a decline on every method. See How Bank Statement Income Is Calculated and One Year of Self-Employment.

If it was assets: stop moving money. Let balances season in one account, document every deposit as it happens, and never accept cash you intend to use. See Using Gift Funds for an FHA Down Payment.

If it was federal debt: start resolving it immediately, because this has the longest lead time of anything on this page. A defaulted federal student loan can generally be rehabilitated or consolidated out of default, and that is months of process. See Collections, Judgments and Federal Debt.

If it was the property: go find a different property, and have your lender vet the next one before you write. Condo project status, water source and roof age are all checkable in advance.

Step 4: do not do these things

Do not apply at six lenders in a panic. It compounds the problem and tells you nothing new. Work with one person who can place a file across many investors.

Do not pay a credit repair company for something you can do yourself. Disputing inaccurate items is a right you already have, for free.

Do not let someone "fix" your file by misstating anything. Occupancy, income, the source of your down payment. That is loan fraud, not creative structuring, and it is a different category of problem from a decline.

Do not assume the decline is permanent. It is a decision by one lender, on one day, on one property, under one set of overlays. Three of those four variables can change.

Do not give up on the timeline you were given. "Come back in two years" is sometimes correct and is frequently just one lender's shelf.

The two questions that reframe everything

"Was this about me, or about the property?"

"Was this the guideline, or your overlay?"

Four possible combinations, and only one of them means you have real work to do. Most declined borrowers never ask either question, and so they never find out they were already approvable somewhere.

Why bring a declined file to us

  • A decline is a data point, not a verdict. The first thing I want is the adverse action notice, because it tells us whether this is a credit problem, an income problem, a property problem or a routing problem. Those have four different answers.
  • Broker model. When the obstacle is one lender's overlay rather than the actual guideline, the fix is moving the file, not fixing you. A single bank cannot do that and will not tell you it exists.
  • We read the documents ourselves, rather than handing them to a processor and waiting.
