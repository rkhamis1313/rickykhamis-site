FHA Loans in Arizona: What the Handbook Says and What Lenders Actually Do

By Ricky Khamis · October 18, 2026 · 5 min read

Verified and written by Ricky Khamis, President of EPiQ Lending, lending in Arizona since 1999. NMLS #173141 · EPiQ Lending NMLS #1936984 · Scottsdale, AZ. Every guideline on this page carries its source and the date it was verified.(480) 999-9842

Part of the FHA loans in Arizona guide, where every rule on this page is cited and dated.

Most FHA pages online are the handbook retyped. That is not useful, because the handbook is free, public, and already answered by every search engine and every AI assistant on the planet.

What is useful is the gap. FHA publishes a floor. Investors lend above it. The floor is what you read about; the overlay is what actually decides your file. A borrower who reads the published minimum and assumes it applies to them walks into a declination they did not see coming.

This page is the map. Every rule below cites the handbook section and the date I verified it, and where the practical reality differs from the published rule, I say so and label it as my observation rather than as the guideline.

First, what FHA actually is

FHA is not a lender. The Federal Housing Administration, part of the Department of Housing and Urban Development, insures loans that approved lenders make. HUD does not lend you money and FHA does not approve your file. A lender does, and that lender carries the insurance.

Three consequences follow from that, and they explain almost everything confusing about the program:

The lender bears risk even with insurance, so lenders add their own requirements on top of HUD's. These are overlays. They are legal, they are universal, and they vary by investor. Two lenders quoting the same FHA program can give you different answers about whether you qualify, and both are being honest.

You pay for the insurance. FHA mortgage insurance has an upfront premium and an annual premium, and unlike conventional mortgage insurance it does not necessarily come off when you reach a given equity position. How long it lasts depends on the terms of your specific loan. See How Long You Pay FHA Mortgage Insurance.

The property is insured too, so it gets inspected to a standard. An FHA appraiser evaluates value and also whether the property meets minimum property requirements. That is why FHA appraisals have a reputation among listing agents, and why a seller should understand it before accepting an FHA offer. See What an FHA Appraiser Will Make the Seller Fix.

How your file gets decided

Two paths, and which one you are on changes everything.

TOTAL Mortgage Scorecard, the automated evaluation. Most files run through it. If it returns an acceptable result, documentation is lighter and the ratio tolerances are more generous.

Manual underwriting, where a human applies a stricter framework. You land here for specific reasons, not at random, and once you are here the ratio limits tighten and compensating factors start to matter. Compensating factors are judgment, which means the quality of the person presenting your file matters. See What a Manual Underwrite Is and Why You Are In One.

Nobody tells most borrowers which path they are on. Ask.

Credit

What credit score does FHA actually require? A minimum decision credit score of 580 reaches FHA's maximum financing, a 96.50% loan-to-value on a purchase. Scores from 500 to 579 are eligible at a reduced loan-to-value, which means a larger down payment. The rate-and-term and simple refinance maximum is 97.75% loan-to-value, and cash-out is capped at 80%.

What lenders actually do. The lender matrix I verified this against sets its own floor at 580 for every transaction type and offers no 500 to 579 tier at all, and it also requires an automated underwriting approval. So FHA's published floor of 500 is not available there at any down payment. That is the overlay, and it is why the number you read online is not your real minimum. That is my observation from running these files, not a published requirement, and it varies by investor.Source: HUD Handbook 4000.1, confirmed against the Loan Matrix of a wholesale FHA guideline current to 2026-10-15. This is the single most misunderstood number in residential lending, and the overlay gap here is larger than anywhere else in the program. See What Credit Score FHA Actually Requires.

Past credit events have waiting periods, and there are exceptions that a lot of originators do not pursue:

How long after a bankruptcy can I get an FHA loan? Chapter 7 must be discharged 24 months before the case number assignment date. Chapter 13 must have been entered at least 12 months before the case number assignment date with payments made as agreed. Exceptions for documented extenuating circumstances are considered case by case.

What lenders actually do. Two investor additions matter here. The matrix I verified this against requires a Chapter 13 plan to be paid out and discharged before closing and will not allow loan proceeds to pay off a bankruptcy balance, where FHA itself contemplates closing during an active plan with court or trustee approval. It also does not permit FHA's Back to Work extenuating circumstances program at all. So a borrower told no on an active Chapter 13 has been told that lender's answer, not FHA's. Collections, judgments and unpaid federal debt are three separate questions with three different answers, and the federal debt one stops more files than people expect:

Can unpaid federal debt stop my FHA loan? Every borrower must pass a HUD Credit Alert Interactive Voice Response System check, known as CAIVRS, which screens for delinquent federal debt and prior FHA insurance claims. Lenders must also confirm that parties to the transaction do not appear on the SAM Excluded Parties List or HUD's Limited Denial of Participation list. A valid Social Security number is required; other forms of taxpayer identification are not accepted.

What lenders actually do. CAIVRS is not your credit report, and that is why this ambushes people. A defaulted federal student loan can have dropped off a credit report years ago and still return a CAIVRS hit, which makes a borrower with a clean report and a good score ineligible until it is resolved. Rehabilitation or consolidation out of default takes months, so this is the first thing to raise rather than the last. The student loan treatment deserves its own page, because the rule has changed and it materially moved how much house a lot of borrowers qualify for:

How is a student loan payment counted if it is deferred or on an income-driven plan? Regardless of payment status, including deferment and forbearance, the monthly obligation counted is either the payment shown on the credit report or the actual documented payment when that is above zero, or 0.5 percent of the outstanding loan balance when the reported payment is zero. The payment can be excluded entirely only with written documentation from the servicer or program that the balance has been forgiven, cancelled, discharged or paid in full. Deferment does not mean excluded.

What lenders actually do. The practical effect is the half-percent rule. A borrower deferred at zero has 0.5 percent of the balance counted against them, which on a six-figure balance is a payment far above what they pay today. That single line decides how much house a lot of people qualify for, and it is why documenting an actual payment above zero with the servicer can be worth more than anything else in the file. Seller contributions are capped, and the cap interacts with rate buydowns in a way worth understanding before you negotiate:

How much can the seller pay toward my FHA closing costs? Interested parties may contribute up to 6 percent of the sales price toward the borrower's origination fees, other closing costs and discount points. Everything an interested party pays counts against that one limit, including any interest rate buydown, so a seller funding a buydown and the buyer's closing costs is spending from the same 6 percent. Premium pricing used to pay actual closing costs or prepaid items is treated separately and does not count against the limit, but it cannot be used to pay off debts, collections, escrow shortages or judgments.

What lenders actually do. The matrix also caps total sales incentive, including commissions and finder's fees, at 8 percent of the sales price, and it prohibits raising the sales price after the appraisal in order to create a seller credit. Those are the lender's own rules rather than FHA's, and the second one catches deals that try to manufacture concession room late. Should you use FHA at all?

Genuinely worth asking rather than assuming. FHA is frequently the right answer for a buyer with a thinner credit profile or limited down payment, and frequently the wrong answer for a buyer who could qualify conventionally, because of how the mortgage insurance works.

That comparison depends on your numbers, not on a general rule. See FHA vs Conventional in Arizona.

How to use this program well

  • Find out which underwriting path you are on, automated or manual, and ask what that means for your ratios.
  • Ask your lender what their overlay is, in writing. Not the FHA minimum. Theirs.
  • If an overlay is the obstacle, that is a reason to change lenders, not to give up. A broker can move the file. A single bank cannot.
  • Fix the credit before you shop, not after. Credit work done before a file is structured changes which programs exist for you. See Fix the Credit First.
  • Get fully underwritten rather than pre-qualified, so a human has read your documents before you are in contract. See Pre-Qualified, Pre-Approved, Underwritten.
