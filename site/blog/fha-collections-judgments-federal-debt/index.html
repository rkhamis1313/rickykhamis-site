Collections, Judgments and Federal Debt: Three Different FHA Problems

By Ricky Khamis · October 18, 2026 · 5 min read

Verified and written by Ricky Khamis, President of EPiQ Lending, lending in Arizona since 1999. NMLS #173141 · EPiQ Lending NMLS #1936984 · Scottsdale, AZ. Every guideline on this page carries its source and the date it was verified.(480) 999-9842

Part of the FHA loans in Arizona guide, where every rule on this page is cited and dated.

Borrowers lump these together. Underwriting does not. They are three distinct issues with three different answers, and treating them as one problem is how a file gets a nasty surprise late.

Collections and charged-off accounts

Do I have to pay off collections and judgments? Collection accounts are evaluated against a cumulative balance threshold rather than individually, and in a community property state the collection accounts of a non-borrowing spouse are included in that cumulative balance and analyzed as part of your ability to pay, unless state law excludes them. Judgments of a non-borrowing spouse in a community property state generally must be resolved. Disputed derogatory accounts are treated differently again, and a non-borrowing spouse's disputed accounts are not counted toward the cumulative balance.

What lenders actually do. Arizona is a community property state, so this matters here in a way it does not in most of the country. A spouse who is not on the loan can still bring collections and judgments into the analysis. If your spouse is staying off the application to protect the file, their derogatory credit may follow it anyway, and that is worth finding out in week one. The important and counterintuitive point: paying an old collection is not automatically the right move.

Depending on the scoring model in use, satisfying an old collection can affect how recent the item appears and occasionally produces a worse score rather than a better one. Which collections to address, in what order, and whether to negotiate is a conversation to have with your lender before you write any checks.

What matters to an underwriter here is generally the aggregate picture and the recency, not the existence of a single old item. Medical collections are frequently viewed differently from consumer collections. Ask how yours are being treated rather than assuming the worst or the best.

What to do:

  • Get the full report through a lender, with a real analysis, not a consumer app.
  • Ask which items are actually affecting the decision, and which are noise.
  • Challenge items that are inaccurate or unverifiable through the bureau dispute process. That is weeks of work, sometimes two rounds, and it has to start before you shop. See Fix the Credit First.
  • Do not pay anything on impulse. Ask first.

One caution on disputes: lenders can see dispute flags on a report, and an account under active dispute can itself create an underwriting condition. Timing matters, which is another reason to do this early rather than during escrow.

Judgments

A judgment is a court ruling that you owe money, and it is a more serious item than a collection because it is enforceable.

What underwriting generally wants to see is either that it is satisfied, or that it is under a documented payment arrangement with a history of payments actually made. A judgment sitting unaddressed is a harder problem than one being paid under an agreement.

Two Arizona-specific points worth knowing:

A recorded judgment can be a lien against real property, which makes it a title issue as well as a credit issue. That means it can surface in the title commitment independently of your credit report, and it has to be resolved before closing. Title problems need weeks, not days. See A Realistic Escrow Timeline.

A payment arrangement needs documentation and a track record. An agreement signed last week is weaker than one with six months of payments behind it. If you know you have a judgment, start the arrangement now rather than when you are in contract.

Unpaid federal debt, which is the one that ambushes people

Can unpaid federal debt stop my FHA loan? Every borrower must pass a HUD Credit Alert Interactive Voice Response System check, known as CAIVRS, which screens for delinquent federal debt and prior FHA insurance claims. Lenders must also confirm that parties to the transaction do not appear on the SAM Excluded Parties List or HUD's Limited Denial of Participation list. A valid Social Security number is required; other forms of taxpayer identification are not accepted.

What lenders actually do. CAIVRS is not your credit report, and that is why this ambushes people. A defaulted federal student loan can have dropped off a credit report years ago and still return a CAIVRS hit, which makes a borrower with a clean report and a good score ineligible until it is resolved. Rehabilitation or consolidation out of default takes months, so this is the first thing to raise rather than the last. This is the item I most want you to take away from this page, because it is the one borrowers never anticipate.

FHA files are screened against a federal database of delinquent federal debt and prior claims. It is not your credit report, and a delinquent federal obligation can make you ineligible regardless of how good your credit looks.

What shows up there:

  • A defaulted federal student loan
  • A prior FHA claim, meaning a previous FHA loan that resulted in an insurance claim
  • Delinquent federal taxes, in some circumstances
  • Other delinquent federal debt, including certain federal business obligations

Why it ambushes people: a defaulted federal student loan can be years old, long since fallen off a credit report, and still show in this screening. A borrower with a clean report and a good score can be ineligible and have no idea why.

What to do:

Raise it on the first call if there is any chance it applies to you. A defaulted student loan in your twenties, a property you lost that had an FHA loan, an unresolved tax matter. Say so. An originator who knows can check and plan; one who finds out at underwriting cannot.

Resolution is possible and it takes time. A defaulted federal student loan can generally be rehabilitated or consolidated out of default, and once resolved and reflected, eligibility can follow. That is months of process, not weeks. Start it now.

Get documentation of the resolution. Showing it is resolved is a separate task from resolving it, and underwriting needs the proof.

How these interact with the rest of your file

Any of the three can route you to a manual underwrite, where ratio limits tighten and compensating factors matter. If you have credit issues, assume you are headed there and prepare accordingly: documented housing history, real reserves after closing, and no discretionary revolving debt. See What a Manual Underwrite Is.

If there is also a past bankruptcy or foreclosure in the picture, the waiting period is a separate question from these items, and both have to clear. See Getting an FHA Loan After a Bankruptcy or Foreclosure.

The order of operations

  1. Get a lender to pull your credit with a real analysis, early, before you shop.
  2. Ask whether the federal debt screening is likely to be an issue for you, and disclose anything that might show.
  3. If a federal debt exists, start resolving it immediately. Longest lead time of anything on this page.
  4. Ask which credit items actually matter to the decision before you pay anything.
  5. Dispute what is genuinely inaccurate, and allow weeks.
  6. Address judgments with a documented arrangement, and know that a recorded judgment is also a title issue.
  7. Build reserves and document your rent history, because you are probably going to need compensating factors.
  8. Then go shopping. Sixty to ninety days of this work is the difference between having options and taking what is left.
