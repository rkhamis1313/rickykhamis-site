Scottsdale New Construction Loans: What Buyers Need to Know Before Building or Buying New Homes

By Ricky Khamis · May 28, 2026 · 4 min read

Buying New Construction in Scottsdale Arizona

New construction homes are everywhere in Scottsdale, Arizona.

Buyers are drawn to:

  • Modern layouts
  • Energy-efficient features
  • Builder incentives
  • Customization opportunities

But here’s what many buyers do not realize.

Buying a new construction home is completely different from buying a resale property.

Different contracts.

Different timelines.

Different financing strategies.

After more than 20 years helping buyers navigate Scottsdale real estate, one thing is clear:

The buyers who win with new construction understand the mortgage strategy before walking into the builder’s sales office.

Why Financing Matters With New Construction

Many buyers assume financing a new build is easier.

Not always.

Builders often have preferred lenders and incentive structures.

Sometimes those incentives are valuable.

Sometimes they are not.

The key is understanding the total financial picture—not just the headline incentive.

At KhamisGroup.com, we help buyers compare financing options objectively before making decisions.

Types of New Construction Loans in Scottsdale

The right loan depends on the property, builder, and construction stage.

Conventional Loans for New Construction

Most Scottsdale new construction homes use conventional financing.

Benefits include:

  • Competitive interest rates
  • Low down payment options
  • Flexible loan terms

This works well for buyers purchasing completed or near-completed homes.

Jumbo Loans for Luxury New Construction

Scottsdale luxury communities often require jumbo financing.

Especially in:

  • North Scottsdale
  • Golf course communities
  • Luxury gated developments

Jumbo loans may require:

  • Larger cash reserves
  • Stronger credit profiles
  • More detailed income documentation

But they also create opportunities for buyers purchasing premium homes in Scottsdale’s luxury market.

FHA and VA Loans for New Construction

Government-backed financing can also work for new construction homes.

Including:

  • FHA loans
  • VA loans

These programs may offer:

  • Lower down payment requirements
  • Flexible qualification standards
  • Competitive financing terms

However, the builder and property must meet specific program guidelines.

Builder Incentives and Mortgage Strategy

This is one of the biggest areas buyers misunderstand.

Builders often advertise incentives like:

  • Closing cost credits
  • Free upgrades
  • Mortgage rate buydowns

Sounds great.

But buyers need to compare the total loan structure carefully.

Sometimes incentives are tied to:

  • Higher mortgage rates
  • Less competitive loan terms
  • Limited financing flexibility

Smart buyers compare long-term costs—not just upfront perks.

This is where working with Ricky Khamis helps buyers avoid expensive mistakes.

Mortgage Rate Buydowns on New Construction

Builders frequently use temporary mortgage buydowns to improve affordability.

Common examples include:

  • 2-1 buydown structures
  • Seller-paid rate reductions
  • Temporary lower monthly payments

This can help buyers:

  • Reduce early payment pressure
  • Increase affordability
  • Preserve liquidity and reserves

In Scottsdale’s higher-price-point market, this strategy has become increasingly common.

Locking Your Interest Rate During Construction

Timing matters with new construction financing.

If the property will not be completed for several months, buyers may need extended rate locks.

This helps protect against rising mortgage rates during construction.

But extended locks may include:

  • Additional fees
  • Specific qualification requirements
  • Timeline restrictions

Understanding these details upfront is critical.

Down Payment Requirements for New Construction

Your required down payment depends on:

  • Loan type
  • Occupancy type
  • Property price
  • Credit profile

Typical ranges include:

Conventional Loans

  • Often 3%–20%+ down

Jumbo Loans

  • Commonly 10%–20%+ down

FHA Loans

  • As low as 3.5% down

VA Loans

  • Potentially no down payment for eligible veterans

The smartest strategy depends on your overall financial goals—not just minimum requirements.

New Construction Timelines and Mortgage Planning

New construction timelines work differently from resale transactions.

Delays happen regularly due to:

  • Material shortages
  • Permit delays
  • Construction scheduling
  • Final inspections

Buyers need mortgage strategies built around flexibility.

Especially if timelines shift.

This is why communication between the lender, builder, and Realtor matters heavily.

Common New Construction Mistakes Buyers Make

These mistakes happen constantly.

Using the Builder Lender Without Comparing Options

Always compare the total financing structure.

Ignoring Future Payment Changes

Understand future taxes, HOA fees, and insurance adjustments.

Making Large Purchases During Construction

Do not change your financial profile before closing.

Waiting Too Long to Get Pre-Approved

The strongest buyers prepare early.

Tips for Realtors Working With New Construction Buyers

If you’re a Realtor in Scottsdale, Arizona, understanding new construction financing gives you a huge advantage.

It helps buyers:

  • Understand builder incentives
  • Compare financing correctly
  • Structure stronger negotiations
  • Avoid financing surprises

That creates smoother closings and stronger client trust.

FAQ: Scottsdale New Construction Loans

Can you use FHA or VA loans for new construction homes?

Yes. FHA and VA financing may be available if the builder and property meet program requirements.

Should buyers use the builder’s preferred lender?

Not automatically. Buyers should compare the total loan structure, rates, and long-term costs carefully.

What are builder incentives?

Builder incentives can include closing cost credits, upgrades, or mortgage rate buydowns designed to improve affordability.

Why do new construction buyers need extended rate locks?

Because construction timelines can last several months, extended locks help protect buyers from rising interest rates during the build process.
