How Listing Agents Should Market the Payment, Not the Price

By Ricky Khamis · October 9, 2026 · 4 min read

A structured buydown that nobody sees is a seller's money spent on nothing. I watch this happen constantly: the concession gets negotiated, it goes into the agent remarks as a line nobody reads, and the listing keeps competing on price against a builder community advertising a rate on a billboard.

This is the playbook for actually using it.

Step 1: bring the lender to the listing appointment

Not after. At it.

Sellers decide their pricing strategy in that meeting, and the strategy they decide on almost always defaults to price because price is the only lever anybody put on the table. Walking in with a payment comparison already run changes what gets decided.

What to bring:

  • The payment a buyer faces at the current price and today's market rate.
  • The payment after the reduction the seller is already thinking about. Let them see how little it moves.
  • The payment with the same dollars pointed at a 2-1 or 3-2-1 buydown.
  • The year four payment, stated plainly, so nobody feels misled later.

Hand the seller the sheet. Let the arithmetic argue. See Seller Concession vs Price Reduction.

Step 2: get the rate locked before the listing goes live

An advertised payment calculated from an assumed rate is a liability. An advertised payment calculated from a rate locked to the address is a fact.

That ordering is the entire reason List & Lock exists. Lock first, list second, advertise a real number.

Step 3: write the number into the public remarks, not the agent remarks

Buyers do not read agent remarks. Portals do not surface agent remarks. If the payment story lives there, it reaches other agents and nobody else.

Lead the public description with it. The structure that works:

Ask about the seller-paid rate buydown on this home. First year payment of approximately $X,XXX per month, then $X,XXX, then $X,XXX from year three. Four bedrooms, three baths, [the rest of the house].

The payment comes first because the payment is the differentiator. The granite comes later.

Step 4: put it in every photo set and every sign

  • First photo caption or a photo overlay carrying the rate. The first image is the only one a large share of buyers ever see.
  • Rider on the sign. "Seller Paid Rate Buydown" or the rate itself. Drive-by traffic in this market is comparing your yard to a builder's banner.
  • Open house flyer with the payment ladder across the top, year by year, and the disclosure line at the bottom. See Advertising a Payment Without Creating a Compliance Problem.
  • Social posts that lead with the number. Not "just listed." Never "just listed." The rate.

Step 5: brief the showing agents

Every agent who shows the house will be asked by their buyer what the catch is. If they cannot answer, the answer they give is a shrug and the buyer moves on.

Give them two sentences they can repeat:

The seller is funding a buydown, so the first year payment is roughly $X,XXX. The buyer was underwritten at the full note rate, so there is no payment they have not already been approved for.

That second sentence kills the objection before it forms. See What Happens When the Buydown Ends.

Step 6: have a lender who answers the phone on a Saturday

A buyer standing in the kitchen at an open house with a question about the buydown is the warmest lead that listing will ever produce. If the answer is "I will have my lender call you Monday," the lead is cold by Monday.

Put a direct line on the flyer. Make sure it is answered.

What not to do

Do not advertise a rate you have not confirmed is available. It will not be available when somebody calls, and you will have created a problem rather than a lead.

Do not quote a payment without the disclosure. Payment advertising has rules. They are manageable and they are not optional.

Do not bury the year four payment. A buyer who discovers the step up in underwriting is a buyer who walks and tells people why.

Do not use this to prop up a mispriced house. If the comparables say you are high, you are high. A buydown on an overpriced listing produces a stale listing with a buydown.

The underlying point

Builders in the north valley are not outselling resale inventory because their houses are better. They are outselling it because they figured out that buyers shop payments and sellers advertise prices, and they are the only ones answering the question the buyer is actually asking.

Any resale listing can answer it too. Almost none do.
