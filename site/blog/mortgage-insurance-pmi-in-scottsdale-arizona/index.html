Mortgage Insurance (PMI) in Scottsdale, Arizona: How It Works, What It Costs, and How to Remove It

By Ricky Khamis · May 1, 2026 · 7 min read

If you are buying a home in Scottsdale, Arizona, with less than 20% down, you will hear about mortgage insurance.

PMI.

Some buyers panic when they see it.

Some buyers avoid buying because they think PMI is a ripoff.

Some buyers accept it without understanding how to control it.

Here is the truth.

PMI is not “good” or “bad.” It is a tool. And in Scottsdale, using PMI strategically can help you buy sooner, keep cash reserves, and win deals without waiting years to save a bigger down payment.

After more than two decades in the mortgage business, Ricky Khamis has helped buyers, investors, and Realtors structure loans to reduce PMI, eliminate it faster, or avoid it altogether when the strategy supports it.

Let’s make it clear.

What Is PMI?

PMI stands for Private Mortgage Insurance.

It is usually required on conventional loans when you put down less than 20% on a primary residence.

PMI protects the lender, not the borrower.

That is why it exists.

But you still need to understand how it affects your payment and your long-term cost.

In Scottsdale, Arizona, where price points can be higher, PMI can be a meaningful monthly number. The good news is you have options.

PMI vs FHA Mortgage Insurance in Scottsdale

Buyers often mix these up.

They are not the same.

Conventional PMI

Conventional PMI is tied to conventional loans.

Key points:

  • It can often be removed later
  • The cost depends heavily on credit score and down payment
  • There are different structures, including monthly PMI and upfront options

FHA Mortgage Insurance

FHA has mortgage insurance, too, but it works differently.

FHA often includes:

  • Upfront mortgage insurance premium (1.75% of the loan amount)
  • Monthly mortgage insurance (typically 0.55% annually)

FHA mortgage insurance can be harder to remove and often requires refinancing to eliminate it, depending on the down payment and loan terms.

This is why choosing between conventional and FHA should be a strategy decision, not a guess.

When PMI Is Actually a Smart Strategy

This is the part most buyers miss.

Sometimes PMI is the better move.

Here is when PMI can make sense in Scottsdale, Arizona.

You Want to Buy Sooner

If home prices keep moving and you have the income to support the payment, waiting to save 20% can cost more than paying PMI for a period of time.

Time matters.

You Want to Keep Cash Reserves

In Scottsdale, keeping reserves can be smart.

Especially if you are:

  • Moving from out of state
  • Buying in an HOA community
  • Planning upgrades or renovations
  • Running a business and want liquidity

A strong buyer is not just a buyer who closes.

It is a buyer who is stable after closing.

You Are Building a Long-Term Plan

Many buyers use PMI as a temporary bridge.

They buy with less down.

They build equity.

They improve credit.

Then they remove PMI or refinance .

That is a real plan.

How to Avoid PMI When Buying in Scottsdale

Option 1: Put 20% Down

The classic approach. No PMI required.

Option 2: VA Loan

If you are an eligible veteran or active-duty service member, a VA loan may require no PMI and no down payment.

Option 3: Lender-Paid PMI

Some lenders offer lender-paid PMI in exchange for a slightly higher interest rate. You avoid monthly PMI, but the cost is built into the rate.

Option 4: Piggyback Loan (80-10-10)

An 80-10-10 structure uses a primary mortgage for 80%, a second loan for 10%, and 10% down. This avoids PMI on the first loan.

Option 5: Single Premium PMI

Some programs allow you to pay PMI as a one-time upfront premium at closing. This eliminates monthly PMI but requires more cash at closing.

How to Lower PMI on a Conventional Loan

If you want to reduce PMI cost, focus on the levers you can control.

Improve Your Credit Score

Credit drives PMI pricing.

Even a modest credit score improvement can reduce PMI and improve the interest rate.

Increase the Down Payment Strategically

A small increase in down payment can sometimes reduce PMI significantly.

This is where we run scenarios.

Not guesses.

Choose the Right PMI Structure

Some loans allow:

  • Monthly PMI
  • Single premium PMI (paid upfront)
  • Lender-paid PMI (built into pricing)

Each option has tradeoffs.

The right one depends on your timeline and goals.

How to Remove PMI in Scottsdale

This is the payoff.

PMI is not always forever.

Automatic Removal at a Certain Equity Point

Most conventional loans remove PMI automatically when you reach 78% loan-to-value based on the original amortization schedule, assuming payments are on time.

Request Removal Based on Equity

You may be able to request PMI cancellation when your loan balance reaches 80% of the home’s original value.

Sometimes this requires:

  • A new appraisal
  • Proof of improvements
  • A payment history review

In Scottsdale, appreciation and smart renovations can accelerate this timeline.

Refinance to Eliminate PMI

If your home value has increased or your loan balance has dropped enough, refinancing into a new loan without PMI may be an option.

You compare:

  • Refinance costs
  • New rate
  • Monthly savings
  • How long will you keep the home

PMI Tips for Scottsdale Realtors

Realtors in Scottsdale, Arizona, should understand PMI because it affects affordability and offers strength.

Here is what helps:

  • Explain to buyers that PMI is not the same as “wasting money.”
  • Encourage buyers to get real payment scenarios early
  • Structure offers knowing the buyer’s true monthly comfort level
  • Help buyers evaluate whether buying sooner is smarter than waiting

When buyers understand PMI, they act with confidence.

Confidence closes deals.

Frequently Asked Questions: PMI in Scottsdale, Arizona

What is PMI on a mortgage in Scottsdale?

PMI stands for Private Mortgage Insurance. It is typically required on conventional loans when you put less than 20% down. PMI protects the lender, not the borrower, but it allows buyers to purchase with less upfront cash.

How much does PMI cost in Scottsdale, Arizona?

PMI typically costs 0.2–1.5% of the loan amount annually, depending on credit score and down payment. On a $500,000 loan with 5% down and strong credit, PMI might cost $80–$200/month.

How do I remove PMI on my Scottsdale mortgage?

PMI on conventional loans can be removed automatically when you reach 78% loan-to-value based on the original schedule, or you can request cancellation at 80% LTV. You may also refinance to eliminate it if home values have increased.

Is PMI the same as FHA mortgage insurance?

No. PMI applies to conventional loans. FHA has its own mortgage insurance that includes an upfront premium and monthly charges. FHA mortgage insurance is often harder to remove and may require refinancing.

How can I avoid PMI in Scottsdale?

You can avoid PMI by putting 20% down, using a VA loan if eligible, choosing lender-paid PMI, using a piggyback loan structure, or paying single premium PMI upfront at closing.

Does PMI affect my ability to qualify for a mortgage in Scottsdale?

Yes. PMI adds to your monthly payment, which affects your debt-to-income ratio. This is why lenders include PMI in the full payment calculation during underwriting.

Is PMI worth it in Scottsdale, Arizona?

It depends on your situation. If home prices are rising and you want to buy sooner while keeping reserves, PMI can be a smart short-term cost. The key is having a plan to remove it.

Can I deduct PMI on my taxes?

PMI tax deductibility has varied by year and is subject to income limits. Consult a tax professional for current rules regarding your specific situation.
